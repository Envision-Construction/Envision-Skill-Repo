## Intestate succession

- **Tex. Est. Code § 201.002(b)** · When an intestate leaves a spouse and descendants, the estate other than the community estate divides so the spouse takes one-third of the personal estate and a life estate in one-third of the land; the descendants take the remaining two-thirds of the personal estate, the other two-thirds of the land, and the remainder after the life estate. "(3) the surviving spouse is entitled to a life estate in one-third of the person's land, with the remainder descending to the person's child or children and the descendants of a child or children." https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/ES/htm/ES.201.htm (verified 2026-09-16; mirror current through 89th Leg. 2nd C.S. 2025; added Acts 2009, 81st Leg., ch. 680 (H.B. 2502), eff. Jan. 1, 2014, no later amendment) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. Est. Code § 201.003(b)-(c)** · The deceased spouse's community estate passes to the surviving spouse if no child or other descendant survives, or if every surviving child and descendant is also a child or descendant of the surviving spouse; if any surviving descendant is not the surviving spouse's, the deceased spouse's undivided one-half community interest passes to the deceased spouse's children or other descendants (taking only the share § 201.101 allows). In every case the community estate passes charged with the debts against it. "the deceased spouse's undivided one-half interest in the community estate passes to the deceased spouse's children or other descendants." https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/ES/htm/ES.201.htm (verified 2026-09-16; last amended Acts 2019, 86th Leg., ch. 1141 (H.B. 2782), § 5, eff. Sept. 1, 2019; mirror current through 89th Leg. 2nd C.S. 2025) [VERIFIED]

## Wills: execution, probate, contest

- **Tex. Est. "attested by two or more credible witnesses who are at least 14 years of age and who subscribe their names to the will in their own handwriting in the testator's presence." https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/ES/htm/ES.251.htm (verified 2026-09-16; last amended Acts 2017, 85th Leg., ch. 844 (H.B. 2271), § 15, eff. - **Tex. Est. Code § 256.003(a)-(b)** · A will may not be admitted to probate after the fourth anniversary of the testator's death unless the applicant proves it was not in default; letters testamentary may not issue on a late-probated will unless the application was filed within four years. "a will may not be admitted to probate after the fourth anniversary of the testator's death unless it is shown by proof that the applicant for the probate of the will was not in default" https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/ES/htm/ES.256.htm (verified 2026-09-16; amended Acts 2015 (S.B. 995) and Acts 2017 (H.B. 2271), eff. ## Marital property

- **Tex. Fam. Code §§ 3.002, 3.003** · Community property is all property, other than separate property, acquired by either spouse during marriage; property possessed by either spouse during or on dissolution of marriage is presumed community, and separate character must be proved by clear and convincing evidence. "(a) Property possessed by either spouse during or on dissolution of marriage is presumed to be community property. (b) The degree of proof necessary to establish that property is separate property is clear and convincing evidence." https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/FA/htm/FA.3.htm (verified 2026-09-16; both added Acts 1997, 75th Leg., ch. 7, eff. - **Tex. Fam. "The partition or exchange of property may also provide that future earnings and income arising from the transferred property shall be the separate property of the owning spouse." https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/FA/htm/FA.4.htm (verified 2026-09-16; §§ 4.102 and 4.104 last amended Acts 2005, ch. 477 (H.B. 202), eff. ## Sponsor angles

Each angle rests only on a VERIFIED bullet above. Mechanism, authority, the counterparty's best counter, what defeats it.

- **Death buy-sell survives the estate.** A deceased founder's estate, spouse, heirs, and devisees hold only an assignee's economic interest in the portfolio LLC and cannot vote, manage, or become members; the company agreement's death-triggered purchase right is statutorily preserved. Authority: BOC § 101.1115(a)(2), (b); § 101.108(b)(2). Counter: the surviving spouse asserts a community one-half in the interest under Fam. Code § 3.003 and argues the decedent could bind only the decedent's half; the estate attacks the buy-sell price as a forfeiture. Defeats it: a company agreement with no death trigger, or a founder who never signed the version containing it.
- **Four-year and two-year diligence gates on inherited equity.** Title to founder equity passing under a will is unsettled until probate is opened within four years of death and the two-year contest window has run; after four years without probate, heirs rather than devisees hold. Authority: Est. Code §§ 256.003(a)-(b), 256.204(a). Counter: a late applicant proves it was "not in default"; a contestant pleads forgery or fraud, which runs from discovery, not admission. Defeats it: the discovery clause, which keeps a fraud-based contest open past the second anniversary, so a transfer rep on inherited equity carries that residual.
- **The 120-day claim answer.** A sponsor or portfolio company holding an indemnity, note, or clawback claim against a deceased founder must state the nature and amount of the claim to the independent executor before the 121st day after receiving a § 308.054 notice or lose it; presenting the claim does not toll limitations. Authority: Est. Code §§ 403.055, 403.057. Counter: the executor disputes the receipt date to shorten the window. Defeats it: an unlogged receipt date; calendar it from the day the notice arrives.
- **Community presumption on carry, cured by partition.** Carried interest, promote, and founder equity acquired during marriage are presumptively community and the founder carries a clear-and-convincing tracing burden; a writing signed by both spouses partitions the carry and its future distributions into separate property with no consideration required. Authority: Fam. Code §§ 3.002, 3.003, 4.102, 4.104, 4.105. Counter: the other spouse pleads involuntariness or unconscionability with nondisclosure under § 4.105; a preexisting creditor pleads § 4.106(a) fraud. Defeats it: an unsigned or one-signature writing, or a signing without disclosure of the carry's value.
- **Directed trust for trust-held sponsor equity.** A founder names an investment advisor to direct hold, vote, sell, and valuation decisions on trust-held LP or LLC interests while a corporate trustee administers; the trustee following direction answers only for its own wilful misconduct and owes no duty to monitor or warn. Authority: Prop. Code § 114.0031(a)(2), (e)-(h). Counter: beneficiaries sue the advisor directly as the fiduciary on valuation and concentration. Defeats it: trust terms that do not "prescribe" the direction, or an advisor whose only power is remove-and-appoint, which is not a fiduciary role and directs nothing.
- **No Texas self-settled shield.** A founder's self-settled spendthrift trust holding sponsor equity stays reachable by the founder's creditors, including a portfolio-company lender on a personal guaranty. Authority: Prop. Code § 112.035(d). Counter: the founder argues the retained power is only a non-settlor trustee's tax reimbursement or a third party's power of appointment, which (d)(1)-(2) exclude. Defeats it: any beneficial interest retained by the settlor beyond those two carve-outs.
- **300-year hold for carry and LP positions.** A Texas trust with an effective date on or after September 1, 2021 may hold fund interests for 300 years; a pre-2021 trust reaches the same period only by instrument opt-in; a direction to retain real property is capped at 100 years. Authority: Prop. Code § 112.036(c)(1), (d), (f). Counter: a remainderman argues a pre-2021 trust lacks the opt-in language and the older period controls. Defeats it: silence in a pre-2021 instrument.
- **No state death-tax clearance at closing.** Transferring equity out of a Texas founder's estate involves no state inheritance-tax return, lien, or clearance, and the constitution now bars the legislature from creating one. Authority: Tex. Const. art. VIII, § 26(a); S.B. 752 (2015). Counter: none at the state level; federal estate and GST tax control. Defeats it: nothing in § 26(b) reaches entity interests; the subsection preserves only a § 29(b) real-property transfer tax, the motor-vehicle gift tax, and ad valorem tax.
- **Forum lock in statutory-probate-court counties.** A dispute over a deceased founder's equity in a county with a statutory probate court belongs in that court, contested or not; related causes of action go there too unless jurisdiction is concurrent under § 32.007 or with another court. Authority: Est. Code § 32.005(a)-(b), §§ 32.006-.007. Counter: the estate files a trust-centered claim in district court under § 32.007 concurrence, or invokes § 32.005(b) to keep the court from supervising the independent executor. Defeats it: a district-court filing that a party attacks under the exclusivity clause before the merits.
- **Series LLC silos hold only with the paperwork.** A family holding company can wall each portfolio investment inside a protected or registered series so one series' creditors cannot reach another's assets. Authority: BOC § 101.602(a)-(c). Counter: the creditor shows commingled records, a company agreement silent on the limitation, or a certificate of formation without notice, and the shield never attached. Defeats it: any one of the (b)(1)-(3) conditions missing, or, for a registered series, no filed certificate.
- **Wet-ink wills or intestacy.** A founder's will executed by e-signature or remote witnessing fails § 251.051's own-handwriting subscription requirement; Texas enacted no electronic-wills statute through the 89th Legislature's second called session; the equity then passes by intestacy, fragmenting ownership among spouse and descendants. Authority: Est. Code §§ 251.051, 201.002(b), 201.003(b)-(c); 88R SB 1779 history. Counter: the proponent offers the instrument as a holographic will, which § 251.052 exempts from attestation. Defeats it: an instrument wholly in the testator's handwriting.
- **Exculpation floor in founder trusts.** An exculpation clause in a founder or family trust holding sponsor equity protects the trustee only as far as § 114.007(a) allows; to the extent it excuses a breach committed in bad faith, intentionally, or with reckless indifference to a beneficiary's interest, or excuses any profit the trustee derived from a breach, it is unenforceable. Authority: Prop. Code § 114.007(a). Counter: the trustee frames a concentration or valuation decision as a good-faith judgment the clause still covers. Defeats it: evidence of bad faith, intent, reckless indifference, or trustee profit, which no clause can reach.
- **Retention clause for a concentrated position.** The prudent investor rule and the § 117.005 duty to diversify are default rules; the instrument may restrict or eliminate them for carried interest, a GP stake, or a single portfolio company, and a trustee acting in reasonable reliance on that provision is not liable to a beneficiary. Authority: Prop. Code §§ 117.003(b), 117.005. Counter: a beneficiary argues the instrument's language does not reach the specific asset, leaving the diversification duty in force absent a documented special-circumstances determination. Defeats it: an instrument silent on retention and a trustee file with no special-circumstances finding.
- **Legend the founder's shares and price the exit in the agreement.** A transfer restriction conspicuously noted on a corporate founder's certificate (or in the § 3.205 notice for uncertificated shares) is specifically enforceable against heirs and devisees as successors or transferees of the holder; a deceased LLC member's successors hold assignee economics and cannot vote, so a death buy-sell is exercised against a party who cannot block it; and a court may not displace an agreed book-value price with a judicially set "fair value" as a shareholder-oppression remedy. Authority: BOC § 21.213(a)-(b); § 101.108(a)-(b); Cardiac Perfusion Servs., Inc. v. Hughes, No. 13-0014 (Tex. June 27, 2014) (per curiam). Counter: the estate pleads a claim other than shareholder oppression to displace the formula, since Cardiac Perfusion holds only that oppression does not support a court-ordered buyout; a transferee for value without actual knowledge takes free of an unnoted restriction. Defeats it: a missing legend or notice on shares that reach a purchaser for value without actual knowledge.
- **Forum map for the § 32.005 lock.** The statutory-probate-court exclusivity of Est. Code § 32.005 attaches only in the twelve counties where Gov't Code ch. 25 subch. C designates such a court (Bexar, Cameron, Collin, Dallas, Denton, El Paso, Galveston, Harris, Hidalgo, Montgomery, Tarrant, Travis; 25 courts); a deceased founder domiciled elsewhere leaves equity disputes to the county court at law or district court structure. Authority: Gov't Code §§ 25.0171(c), 25.0331(a-1), 25.0451(b), 25.0591(d), 25.0631(b), 25.0731(b), 25.0861(b), 25.1031(c), 25.1101(b), 25.1721(b), 25.2221(c), 25.2291(c); Est. Code § 32.005(a)-(b). Counter: a party invokes probate exclusivity in a county whose "County Court at Law and Probate Court" is a statutory county court under § 25.0221 (Brazoria), which the designation does not reach. Defeats it: domicile outside the twelve counties, or a designation the phrase-search method behind the roster would miss.
- **Confirm the administration is open before taking an executor's signature.** Since September 1, 2025, filing a closing report or notice of closing estate terminates the independent executor's power and cancels the letters, so a sponsor taking an executor's signature on a transfer of founder equity confirms no closing filing precedes it. Authority: S.B. 1335, 89th Leg., R.S., Acts 2025, ch. 818, § 6 (Est. Code § 405.007(b)), § 9. Counter: the executor tenders letters issued before the closing as proof of authority. Defeats it: a signature dated before the closing filing; the verified text speaks only to authority after closing.
- **No-contest clause in the founder trust, with the burden on the contestant.** A forfeiture clause in a trust holding sponsor equity strips the interest of a beneficiary who sues over the trust unless that beneficiary proves, by a preponderance, both just cause and good faith; the settlor cannot draft a stricter standard, since § 111.0035(b)(6) puts § 112.038 outside the reach of the trust terms. Authority: Prop. Code §§ 112.038(a), 111.0035(b)(6). Counter: the contestant carries the preponderance showing of just cause and good faith and keeps the interest. Defeats it: a contest the court finds was brought with just cause and maintained in good faith; the clause then forfeits nothing.
- **Draft the founder trust to the mandatory-rule wall.** Trust terms override the Trust Code except the six § 111.0035(b) items: the § 112.031 requirements, § 114.007's reach over exculpation, limitation periods, the good-faith duty and § 113.151 accounting response owed to irrevocable-trust beneficiaries, the court's powers under §§ 112.054, 113.082, 115.001 and over bonds, disgorgement, and § 114.064 fees, and § 112.038. A quiet-trust or retention design for carried interest works within that list, not around it. Authority: Prop. Code § 111.0035(b). Counter: a beneficiary attacks any term touching a listed item as void to that extent. Defeats it: a term that limits a listed item; the rest of the instrument stands, that clause does not.
- **Allowances come off the top before any estate transfer.** When a deceased founder's effects lack the exempt articles, the court makes a capped allowance in lieu of homestead and in lieu of other exempt property, a fixed-dollar prior charge to size before pricing a purchase of equity from the estate or a note collection against it; read the live § 353.053(b) caps, never this file, before quoting them. Authority: Est. Code § 353.053(a)-(b). Counter: the estate shows the exempt articles exist, so the set-aside runs in kind and no allowance is owed. Defeats it: a homestead and exempt personalty actually present in the estate, which shifts the question from allowance to set-aside.
- **Partition of carry takes the spouse out of the family allowance.** The court fixes a one-year maintenance allowance for the founder's spouse, minor children, and adult incapacitated children with no statutory dollar cap, but a spouse with adequate separate property gets none; a partition agreement that makes the spouse's share of carry or its distributions separate property supplies that property. Authority: Est. Code §§ 353.101(a), (d)(1), 353.102(a)-(c); Fam. Code § 4.102. Counter: the spouse shows the separate property is illiquid or short of a year's maintenance. Defeats it: a partition never signed, or separate property the court finds inadequate.
- **A county-specific statute cannot widen the probate court's reach.** A statutory probate court holds probate jurisdiction under the Estates Code plus the listed Health and Safety Code matters; a county-court statute that tries to add jurisdiction beyond probate, guardianship, mental health, or eminent domain yields to § 25.0021, so a sponsor's stand-alone commercial claim reaches that court only through Estates Code related-matter jurisdiction. Authority: Gov't Code § 25.0021(a)-(b); Est. Code § 32.005(a). Counter: the estate pleads the commercial claim as related to the probate proceeding to pull it under § 32.005(a). Defeats it: a claim the court finds related to the pending estate; the exclusivity clause then takes it anyway.
- **Read the marital-character column on the inventory.** Since September 1, 2025, a Texas inventory states the decedent's marital status and labels each asset separate or community, and the list of claims does the same for each receivable, so a founder's estate inventory states on its face whether sponsor equity is community property; both acts carry the change identically. Authority: S.B. 1448, 89th Leg., R.S., enrolled §§ 5, 6 (Est. Code §§ 309.051(a), 309.052); H.B. 3421, enrolled §§ 4, 5. Counter: the surviving spouse contests the label, since the inventory's characterization is the representative's, not an adjudication. Defeats it: nothing on the face of the inventory binds the spouse; the label is a lead, and the Fam. Code § 3.003 community presumption still governs the character fight.
- **Serve the dead survivor's estate before the court acts on the equity.** Where a founder's heir or spouse survived the founder but has since died, citation in the founder's estate proceeding runs to that person's personal representative or distributees, or by publication if none is determined, with an attorney ad litem available; the section exists only by SB 1448 (ch. 831) and applies to proceedings commenced on or after September 1, 2025. Authority: Est. Code § 51.057; S.B. 1448, 89th Leg., R.S., enrolled §§ 2, 14. Counter: a party attacks an order entered without § 51.057 service as void against the deceased survivor's successors. Defeats it: a proceeding commenced before September 1, 2025, which § 14 leaves under prior law.
