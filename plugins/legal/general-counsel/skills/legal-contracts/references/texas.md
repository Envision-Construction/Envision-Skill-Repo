## Limitations and contractual clocks

- **Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 16.004(a)(1), (3), (4), (5)** · Suit on debt, fraud, breach of fiduciary duty, or specific performance of a real-property conveyance contract must be brought within four years after the cause of action accrues. "A person must bring suit on the following actions not later than four years after the day the cause of action accrues ... (3) debt; (4) fraud; or (5) breach of fiduciary duty." https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-b/chapter-16/subchapter-a/section-16-004/ (verified 2026-09-16; 2025 Texas Statutes mirror, official CP.16 banner reported current through 89th 2nd C.S. 2025; last amended Acts 1999, 76th Leg., ch. 950, eff. Aug. 30, 1999, which added the (a)(5) fiduciary-duty prong) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 16.051** · Every action without an express limitations period, other than recovery of real property, must be brought within four years after accrual; contract claims lacking a specific period fall here. "Every action for which there is no express limitations period, except an action for the recovery of real property, must be brought not later than four years after the day the cause of action accrues." https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-b/chapter-16/subchapter-c/section-16-051/ (verified 2026-09-16; 2025 Texas Statutes mirror; Acts 1985, 69th Leg., ch. 959, eff. Sept. 1, 1985; no amendment since) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 16.070(a)** · Parties may not contractually shorten the time to sue on their agreement to less than two years; a shorter contractual period is void in Texas, subject to the business-sale exception in subsection (b). "a person may not enter a stipulation, contract, or agreement that purports to limit the time in which to bring suit on the stipulation, contract, or agreement to a period shorter than two years." https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-b/chapter-16/subchapter-d/section-16-070/ (verified 2026-09-16; 2025 Texas Statutes mirror; amended Acts 1991, 72nd Leg., ch. 840, Sec. 2, eff. Aug. 26, 1991; the section continues "void in this state") [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 16.070(b)** · The two-year floor does not apply to an agreement for the sale or purchase of a business entity where a party pays or receives, or is obligated to pay or entitled to receive, consideration with an aggregate value of at least $500,000. "the sale or purchase of a business entity if a party ... pays or receives or is obligated to pay or entitled to receive consideration ... having an aggregate value of not less than $500,000." https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-b/chapter-16/subchapter-d/section-16-070/ (verified 2026-09-16; 2025 Texas Statutes mirror; subsection (b) added by Acts 1991, 72nd Leg., ch. 840, Sec. 2, eff. Aug. 26, 1991) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 16.071(a), (e), (f)** · A contractual notice-of-claim condition precedent must be reasonable; a stipulation requiring notice within less than 90 days is void; notice is presumed given unless lack of notice is specifically pleaded under oath (applies to suits under this section or § 16.070); the section does not reach a business-entity sale with consideration of at least $500,000. "is not valid unless the stipulation is reasonable. A stipulation that requires notification within less than 90 days is void." https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-b/chapter-16/subchapter-d/section-16-071/ (verified 2026-09-16; 2025 Texas Statutes mirror; amended Acts 1991, 72nd Leg., ch. 840, Sec. 3, eff. Aug. 26, 1991) [VERIFIED]

## Deceptive Trade Practices Act

- **Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 17.42(a)-(c)** · A consumer's DTPA waiver is void unless in writing and signed by the consumer, the consumer is not in a significantly disparate bargaining position, and the consumer is represented by counsel not directly or indirectly identified, suggested, or selected by the defendant or its agent; the waiver must be conspicuous, in bold-face type of at least 10 points, headed "Waiver of Consumer Rights" or words of similar meaning, and in substantially the statutory form (subsection (d) permits limiting the waiver to specified rights). - **Tex. Bus. & Com. "a cause of action arising from a transaction, a project, or a set of transactions relating to the same project, involving total consideration by the consumer of more than $500,000, other than a cause of action involving a consumer's residence" https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/business-commerce-code/title-2/chapter-17/subchapter-e/section-17-49/ (verified 2026-09-16; 2025 Texas Statutes mirror; section last amended 2011 (S.B. 1353); subsection-level effective date not stated on the page) [VERIFIED]

## Covenants not to compete

- **Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 15.50(a)** · A covenant not to compete is enforceable if ancillary to or part of an otherwise enforceable agreement when made, to the extent its time, geographic, and scope limits are reasonable and impose no greater restraint than necessary to protect the promisee's goodwill or other business interest, subject to subsection (b) (physicians) and § 15.501 (health care practitioners). "ancillary to or part of an otherwise enforceable agreement at the time the agreement is made to the extent that it contains limitations as to time, geographical area, and scope of activity to be restrained that are reasonable" https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/business-commerce-code/title-2/chapter-15/subchapter-e/section-15-50/ (verified 2026-09-16; 2025 Texas Statutes mirror, official BC.15 banner reported current through 89th 2nd C.S. 2025; subsection (a) amended by Acts 2025, 89th Leg., R.S., ch. 816 (S.B. 1318), Sec. 1, eff. Sept. 1, 2025, adding the cross-reference to new § 15.501, which Sec. 2 of the same Act added; TLO history: signed June 20, 2025, effective Sept. 1, 2025) [VERIFIED]

## Choice of law, forum, and venue

- **Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 271.001** · A "qualified transaction" under Chapter 271 is one in which a party pays or receives, or is obligated or entitled to, consideration of at least $1 million, or (subsection (2)) lends, advances, borrows, or receives money or credit of at least $1 million. "'qualified transaction' means a transaction under which a party: (1) pays or receives, or is obligated to pay or is entitled to receive, consideration with an aggregate value of at least $1 million" https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/business-commerce-code/title-9/chapter-271/section-271-001/ (verified 2026-09-16; 2025 Texas Statutes mirror, official banner reported current through 89th 2nd C.S. 2025; added by Acts 2007, 80th Leg., R.S., ch. 885 (H.B. 2278), eff. April 1, 2009; unamended) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 271.005(a)-(b)** · For a qualified transaction, the law of a jurisdiction chosen in a written agreement governs if the transaction bears a reasonable relation to that jurisdiction, and it governs regardless of whether it is contrary to a fundamental or public policy of Texas or any other jurisdiction, subject to the exceptions (a) lists: §§ 271.007, 271.008(b), 271.009, 271.010, 271.011 and Chapter 272. "The law of a particular jurisdiction governs an issue described by this section regardless of whether the application of that law is contrary to a fundamental or public policy of this state or of any other jurisdiction." https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/business-commerce-code/title-9/chapter-271/section-271-005/ (verified 2026-09-16; 2025 Texas Statutes mirror plus enrolled S.B. 1239 at capitol.texas.gov; (a)-(b) unchanged since 2009; Acts 2025, 89th Leg., R.S., ch. 311 (S.B. 1239), Sec. 3, eff. - **DeSantis v. Wackenhut Corp., 793 S.W.2d 670 (Tex. 1990)** · Texas applies Restatement (Second) of Conflict of Laws § 187 to contractual choice-of-law clauses; the chosen law yields where another state has a more significant relationship, a materially greater interest, and its fundamental policy would be contravened; Texas law, not the chosen Florida law, governed the non-compete, which was unenforceable. Below the § 271.001 threshold this is the operative test. "We believe the rule is best formulated in section 187 of the Restatement and will therefore look to its provisions in our analysis of this case." https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2376061/desantis-v-wackenhut-corp/ (verified 2026-09-16; docket C-6617; opinion on rehearing June 6, 1990, withdrawing the July 13, 1988 opinion; followed in Exxon Mobil Corp. v. Drennen, No. 12-0621 (Tex. Aug. 29, 2014, reh'g denied Feb. 27, 2015); for qualified transactions § 271.005(b) displaces the fundamental-policy step) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 272.001(a)-(c)** · In a construction contract concerning Texas real property, a provision making the contract or any conflict under it subject to another state's law, litigation in another state's courts, or arbitration in another state is void as against public policy; to the extent a venue provision is void under (b), and absent a post-dispute stipulation to another venue, the action must be brought in the Texas county where the property is located. "making the contract or agreement or any conflict arising under the contract or agreement subject to another state's law, litigation in the courts of another state, or arbitration in another state, that provision is void as against public policy" https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/business-commerce-code/title-9/chapter-272/section-272-001/ (verified 2026-09-16; 2025 and 2024 Justia mirrors plus enrolled H.B. 2960; Acts 2025, 89th Leg., R.S., ch. 427 (H.B. 2960), Secs. 1-2, eff. ## Fraudulent inducement and anti-reliance

- **Schlumberger Tech. Corp. v. Swanson, 959 S.W.2d 171 (Tex. 1997)** · A release that clearly expresses intent to waive fraudulent-inducement claims, or a clause disclaiming reliance on representations about the specific matters in dispute, can preclude a fraudulent-inducement claim; the next sentence of the opinion says a disclaimer of reliance or merger clause "will not always bar" such a claim. "we hold that a release that clearly expresses the parties' intent to waive fraudulent inducement claims, or one that disclaims reliance on representations about specific matters in dispute, can preclude a claim of fraudulent inducement." https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1785528/schlumberger-technology-corp-v-swanson/ (verified 2026-09-16; docket 95-0355; Dec. 11, 1997; quote at 959 S.W.2d 181; followed in Italian Cowboy (2011) and IBM v. Lufkin, No. 17-0666 (Tex. Mar. 15, 2019); no reversal or overruling found) [VERIFIED]
- **Italian Cowboy Partners, Ltd. v. Prudential Ins. Co. of Am., 341 S.W.3d 323 (Tex. 2011)** · A pure merger clause, without clear and unequivocal language disclaiming reliance or waiving fraudulent-inducement claims, does not negate the reliance element of a fraud claim; the lease language at issue did not do so as a matter of law. "Pure merger clauses, without an expressed clear and unequivocal intent to disclaim reliance or waive claims for fraudulent inducement, have never had the effect of precluding claims for fraudulent inducement." https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/895311/italian-cowboy-partners-ltd-v-prudential-insurance-co-of-america/ (verified 2026-09-16; docket 08-0989; April 15, 2011; relied on in IBM v. Lufkin (Tex. 2019) for the proposition that a merger clause standing alone does not bar fraudulent inducement; no reversal, vacatur, or overruling found) [VERIFIED]

### Context (not independently verified)

- **Forest Oil Corp. v. McAllen, 268 S.W.3d 51 (Tex. 2008)** · In deciding whether a disclaimer of reliance binds, courts weigh whether the terms were negotiated rather than boilerplate and the disputed issue was discussed, whether the complaining party had counsel, whether the deal was arm's length, whether the parties were knowledgeable in business, and whether the release language was clear; the court declined a per se rule. ## Liquidated damages

- **Phillips v. Phillips, 820 S.W.2d 785 (Tex. 1991)** · A liquidated-damages clause is enforceable only if the harm from breach is incapable or difficult of estimation and the amount is a reasonable forecast of just compensation; a clause requiring payment of a multiple of actual damages fails both prongs and is an unenforceable penalty on its face, and the penalty defense need not be pleaded when apparent on the face of the petition. "the court must find: (1) that the harm caused by the breach is incapable or difficult of estimation, and (2) that the amount of liquidated damages called for is a reasonable forecast of just compensation." https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1522113/phillips-v-phillips/ (verified 2026-09-16; docket D-0107; Dec. 11, 1991; reh'g overruled Jan. 29, 1992; quote at 820 S.W.2d 788; still applied in Atrium Med. ## Indemnity and release: express negligence and fair notice

## Sponsor angles

Each angle rests only on a VERIFIED bullet above. Format: mechanism; authority; the counterparty's best counter; what defeats it.

- **Short indemnity survival in the purchase agreement.** Mechanism: a survival period under two years for indemnity claims is enforceable where the deal is a sale or purchase of a business entity with aggregate consideration of at least $500,000. Authority: § 16.070(b). Counter: the seller argues the instrument is not "the sale or purchase of a business entity" as the text reads, or that aggregate consideration as the statute measures it falls under $500,000. Defeats it: either showing, after which § 16.070(a) voids the clause and the two-year floor governs.
- **Claim-notice conditions in service and supply contracts.** Mechanism: a notice-of-claim window of at least 90 days, reasonable on its facts, is a valid condition precedent, and compliance is presumed unless the defendant pleads lack of notice under oath. Authority: § 16.071(a), (e). Counter: the claimant attacks the window as unreasonable or shows it is shorter than 90 days, which voids it. Defeats it: the defending portfolio company fails to plead lack of notice specifically and under oath, and the presumption runs against it.
- **Four-year outer clock on fraud, debt, fiduciary, and residual contract claims.** Mechanism: these claims run four years from accrual, which sets the exposure window a sponsor faces or holds after closing. Authority: §§ 16.004(a), 16.051. Counter: the accrual date. Defeats it: a contractual period no shorter than two years under § 16.070(a), or the (b) carve-out for entity sales of at least $500,000.
- **Fee recovery against organizational counterparties.** Mechanism: on a contract claim, a prevailing claimant recovers reasonable attorney's fees from an LLC, partnership, or other organization, not only from individuals and corporations. Authority: § 38.001(a), (b)(8) as amended by H.B. 1578 (eff. Sept. 1, 2021). Counter: the defendant is one of the four excluded classes. Defeats it: nothing on the text; the exposure runs in reverse, and a portfolio company that loses a contract claim pays the other side's fees.
- **Taking six-figure contracts out of the DTPA.** Mechanism: a written contract with total consideration over $100,000, negotiated with the consumer represented by counsel the defendant did not identify, suggest, or select, and not involving the consumer's residence, is outside the DTPA; over $500,000 no writing or counsel condition applies. Authority: § 17.49(f), (g). Counter: the consumer shows its counsel was suggested by the defendant, that consideration falls under the threshold, or that the transaction involves its residence. Defeats it: an oral or unrepresented deal between $100,000 and $500,000, where only a § 17.42 waiver meeting every formality (writing, signature, no significantly disparate bargaining position, independent counsel, 10-point bold, "Waiver of Consumer Rights" heading, substantially the statutory form) removes DTPA exposure.
- **Delaware or New York law in a $1 million-plus deal.** Mechanism: for a qualified transaction, the chosen law governs even against Texas fundamental policy if the transaction bears a reasonable relation to the chosen jurisdiction. Authority: §§ 271.001, 271.005(a)-(b). Counter: no reasonable relation, or the issue falls under §§ 271.007, 271.008(b), 271.009, 271.010, 271.011, or Chapter 272. Defeats it: consideration under $1 million, where DeSantis's § 187 analysis governs and Texas fundamental policy (non-competes in DeSantis itself) can override the clause; or a Texas construction contract, where § 272.001 voids the foreign-law clause outright.
- **Texas law and county venue in construction forms.** Mechanism: in a construction contract for Texas real property, a clause selecting another state's law, courts, or arbitration seat is void as against public policy, and where the venue clause is void the action goes to the county where the property sits absent a post-dispute stipulation; Envision-side forms select Texas law and that county. Authority: § 272.001(a)-(c) as amended by H.B. 2960. Counter: for a contract entered into or renewed before Sept. 1, 2025, the pre-amendment "voidable" text applies (H.B. 2960 Sec. 4). Defeats it: nothing on the current text for later contracts; the clause is void, not voidable.
- **Routing a large contract dispute to the Business Court.** Mechanism: a consent-to-Business-Court clause plus an amount in controversy over $5 million, excluding interest, statutory and exemplary damages, penalties, fees, and costs, gives the Business Court concurrent jurisdiction over the contract action; joined parties' claims aggregate. Authority: Gov't Code § 25A.004(d)(2), (i). Counter: the plaintiff pleads under $5 million once excluded items are stripped; DTPA and Chapter 15 Subchapter E claims stay out absent supplemental jurisdiction. Defeats it: an insurance contract, which (d)(2) excludes, or a dispute that nets under $5 million.
- **Cutting off seller-fraud claims with a specific anti-reliance clause.** Mechanism: a clause disclaiming reliance on representations about the specific matters later in dispute can preclude fraudulent inducement. Authority: Schlumberger; Italian Cowboy. Counter: the clause is a pure merger or entire-agreement clause, which has never barred fraudulent inducement, or the disclaimer is generic and not tied to the disputed subject. Defeats it: drafting that stops at "this agreement supersedes all prior representations" and never says the party disclaims reliance on any representation outside the agreement.
- **Termination fees that survive a penalty challenge.** Mechanism: a liquidated sum is enforceable where harm was incapable or difficult of estimation at contracting and the sum is a reasonable forecast of just compensation. Authority: Phillips. Counter: the harm was readily estimable, the sum is not a forecast, or the clause is a multiple of actual damages, which is a penalty on its face and needs no pleading. Defeats it: any multiplier drafting (two or three times actual loss).
- **Jury waiver in portfolio-company contracts.** Mechanism: a knowing and voluntary pre-dispute jury waiver is enforceable, and mandamus corrects a trial court that refuses it. Authority: In re Prudential. Counter: the waiver was not knowing and voluntary (buried, uncaptioned, unrepresented signer). Defeats it: the Court left open whether conspicuousness is required, so a non-conspicuous waiver invites a fact fight the sponsor cannot win as a matter of law.
- **Management non-competes in the acquisition.** Mechanism: a covenant ancillary to an otherwise enforceable agreement is enforceable to the extent its time, geography, and scope are reasonable and no broader than the goodwill or other business interest needs. Authority: § 15.50(a). Counter: the covenant is not ancillary, or its limits exceed the protected interest. Defeats it: a promisor who is a physician under § 15.50(b) or, since Sept. 1, 2025, a health care practitioner under § 15.501, whose separate rules are carried as context bullets above and must be pulled live.
- **Guaranties and multi-year commitments.** Mechanism: a founder or seller guaranty, a multi-year service commitment, or a real-property deal binds only when in a writing signed by the party to be charged or its lawfully authorized agent. Authority: § 26.01(a), (b)(2), (4)-(6). Counter: the promise was oral, or the writing is signed only by the other side. Defeats it: relying on an unsigned email chain or an oral side deal from the counterparty.
- **Indemnity for the indemnitee's own negligence.** Mechanism: an indemnity meant to cover the owner's, general contractor's, or buyer's own negligence is enforceable only if it states that intent in specific terms within the four corners of the contract; comparative indemnity is available on the same condition. Authority: Ethyl. Counter: the clause speaks of "any and all claims" without naming the indemnitee's negligence, so it shifts nothing for the indemnitee's own fault. Defeats it: boilerplate that never uses the word negligence in connection with the indemnitee.
- **Pre-dispute releases and waivers in MSAs and site-access agreements.** Mechanism: a release that relieves a portfolio company in advance of liability for its own future negligence is enforceable only if it meets both fair notice requirements, express negligence plus conspicuousness; a clause failing either is unenforceable as a matter of law. Authority: Dresser; Storage & Processors. Counter: the counterparty had actual knowledge of the terms, an affirmative defense the enforcing party must prove; or the clause is ordinary risk shifting outside the doctrine (no-damages-for-delay, insurance-shifting), which Storage & Processors puts beyond fair notice. Defeats it: exculpatory text printed among uniform numbered paragraphs on the back of a form, which Dresser held without effect.
- **Conspicuousness by the statutory markers.** Mechanism: a risk-shifting clause drafted with a capitalized heading at least as large as the surrounding text and an operative sentence in larger or contrasting type or set off by symbols meets the statutory definition, and conspicuousness is a decision for the court, so enforceability can be resolved without a jury. Authority: § 1.201(b)(10)(A)-(B); Dresser (adopting the standard for indemnities and releases). Counter: the heading is in capitals but the operative sentence sits in same-size body text. Defeats it: uniform type with no heading, symbol, or contrast.
- **Negotiated damages waivers against a fraud finding.** Mechanism: a limitation-of-liability clause waiving punitive and consequential damages between sophisticated, counseled parties bargaining at arm's length bars a punitive award for fraud when the claimant sues to enforce rather than rescind. Authority: Bombardier. Counter: the claimant elects rescission, or shows it was unrepresented or the clause was not bargained. Defeats it: a claimant that rescinds and forfeits the bargain to escape the cap; and because the Court adjudicated only the punitive bar, a consequential-damages waiver rests on the same reasoning without a square holding.
- **As-is clauses in asset and real-estate acquisitions.** Mechanism: a freely negotiated as-is agreement, not procured by fraudulent inducement, negates causation on DTPA, fraud, negligence, and good-faith claims because the buyer agreed to make its own appraisal and accept the risk of being wrong. Authority: Prudential. Counter: the buyer pleads fraudulent inducement of the as-is clause itself, which Prudential excludes from the clause's reach. Defeats it: a seller misrepresentation or concealment that induced the clause; the inspection-impairment and adhesion routes are context above and must be pulled live.
- **Warranty disclaimers in supply and equipment forms.** Mechanism: a written disclaimer that names merchantability and is conspicuous excludes the implied warranty of merchantability; a written, conspicuous exclusion, for which general language suffices, excludes the implied warranty of fitness. Authority: § 2.316(b). Counter: the disclaimer omits the word merchantability or is not conspicuous. Defeats it: a lower-case disclaimer buried in body text, which leaves both implied warranties in place; the as-is and examination routes of § 2.316(c) are context above.
- **Reciting the deal value for mandatory venue.** Mechanism: a written agreement whose stated aggregate consideration is $1 million or more is a major transaction, which brings its venue clause under the mandatory-venue section rather than the general venue rules. Authority: § 15.020(a). Counter: the agreement states no aggregate value or a value under $1 million, or the transaction is primarily for personal, family, or household purposes. Defeats it: an agreement that never recites consideration on its face, since the threshold is the stated value; the shall-be-brought rule in (b)-(c) and the H.B. 2960 change to (d)(2) are context above and must be pulled live, and a Texas construction contract's out-of-state venue clause is void under § 272.001 regardless.
- **Commercial consequential-damages exclusions in supply and equipment forms.** Mechanism: in a sale of goods, an exclusion of consequential damages is enforceable unless unconscionable, and the statute itself says a limitation where the loss is commercial is not prima facie unconscionable, so the counterparty's only statutory attack is unconscionability on the facts. Authority: § 2.719(c). Counter: the buyer pleads unconscionability, or characterizes the contract as one for services or a mixed contract outside Chapter 2, where the waiver runs on common-law construction alone. Defeats it: a consumer-goods sale with a personal-injury limitation, which the same subsection makes prima facie unconscionable.
- **Pairing an exclusive repair-or-replace remedy with a free-standing consequential exclusion.** Mechanism: when an exclusive or limited remedy fails of its essential purpose the buyer regains every Chapter 2 remedy, so the consequential-damages exclusion is drafted as an independent clause rather than a rider on the exclusive remedy. Authority: § 2.719(b), (c). Counter: the buyer argues that failure of the exclusive remedy under (b) also voids the separate exclusion under (c). Defeats it: no Supreme Court of Texas opinion located resolves the (b)/(c) interaction, so a seller that cannot perform the repair-or-replace remedy faces a live fight over the exclusion.
- **Negotiated consequential waivers between commercial counterparties, with the carve-out watched.** Mechanism: the Supreme Court of Texas has enforced consequential-damages waivers in commercial supply and transportation agreements to bar plant-repair and equipment-upgrade damages on contract claims and refused to let a negligence theory route around them; this is the square holding the Bombardier angle above records as missing. Authority: El Paso Marketing. Counter: the waiver opens "except as specifically set forth herein" and the contract carries a cover, replacement-cost, or other substitute-remedy clause, which pulls those costs back out of the waiver. Defeats it: a carve-out that broad drafted alongside a substitute-remedy clause; the carve-out language decides the case.
- **Presentment before any fee-bearing contract suit.** Mechanism: fees under Chapter 38 require an attorney-represented claimant, presentment of the claim to the opposing party or its duly authorized agent, and no tender of the just amount owed within 30 days after presentment; no particular form is required, so a dated letter or email insisting on performance suffices. Authority: § 38.002(1)-(3); Jones v. Kelley. Counter: the defendant shows no presentment, presentment to someone other than the party or its duly authorized agent, or a tender within the 30 days. Defeats it: filing suit with no prior demand, or with none that can be proved; keep the transmittal record and its date.
- **Contract clocks run from breach.** Mechanism: a breach-of-contract claim accrues when the contract is breached, and the discovery rule rarely defers accrual because diligent contracting parties, who are not fiduciaries to each other, should find the breach within four years; earn-out, indemnity, and purchase-price-adjustment claims against a portfolio company run from the breach date under §§ 16.004(a)(3) or 16.051, not from when the counterparty noticed. Authority: Via Net; §§ 16.004(a), 16.051. Counter: the claimant pleads the discovery rule and shows the breach was inherently undiscoverable and objectively verifiable. Defeats it: the rare contract breach that meets both elements; the Court did not hold the rule can never apply to a contract claim.
- **Defending late-added fraud and fiduciary claims after closing.** Mechanism: where the discovery rule applies, accrual is deferred only until the claimant knew or with reasonable diligence should have known of facts leading to discovery of the wrongful act, not until it learned which defendant caused the injury, even where the defendant is a fiduciary; a claimant that knew of its loss more than four years before naming the sponsor is barred under § 16.004(a)(4)-(5). Authority: Marcus & Millichap v. Triex. Counter: the claimant pleads and proves fraudulent concealment, which tolls until facts would prompt a reasonably prudent person to inquire (context bullet above, pull live). Defeats it: facts within four years of suit that a diligent claimant could not have connected to any wrongful act.
