## Usury ceilings and rate computation

- **Tex. Fin. Code § 302.001(b), (c)** · Absent another statutory ceiling, the maximum lawful interest in Texas is 10 percent a year; a higher rate is usurious and triggers Chapter 305 penalties; subsection (c) aggregates all interest and amortizes it by the actuarial method over the stated term. "The maximum rate or amount of interest is 10 percent a year except as otherwise provided by law. A greater rate of interest than 10 percent a year is usurious unless otherwise provided by law." https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/finance-code/title-4/subtitle-a/chapter-302/subchapter-a/section-302-001/ (verified 2026-09-16; Justia 2025 Texas Statutes, identical to official text current through 89th 2nd C.S., 2025; last amended Acts 2001, ch. 916, eff. 2001-09-01) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. Fin. Code § 302.002** · Where no interest was agreed, a creditor may charge 6 percent a year legal interest starting the 30th day after the amount is due; any agreed compensation constituting interest fixes the rate produced by that amount even if unstated. "may charge and receive from the obligor legal interest at the rate of six percent a year on the principal amount of the credit extended beginning on the 30th day after the date on which the amount is due" https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/finance-code/title-4/subtitle-a/chapter-302/subchapter-a/section-302-002/ (verified 2026-09-16; Justia 2025 Texas Statutes, identical to official text current through 89th 2nd C.S., 2025; last amended Acts 1999, ch. 62, eff. 1999-09-01) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. Fin. Code § 303.009(a)-(c)** · Chapter 303 optional ceilings float with a floor of 18 percent a year and a cap of 24 percent, except that for business, commercial, investment, or similar-purpose credit the cap is 28 percent a year. "For a contract made, extended, or renewed under which credit is extended for a business, commercial, investment, or similar purpose, the limitation on the ceilings determined by those computations is 28 percent a year." https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/finance-code/title-4/subtitle-a/chapter-303/subchapter-a/section-303-009/ (verified 2026-09-16; Justia 2025 Texas Statutes, identical to official text current through 89th 2nd C.S., 2025; last amended Acts 2011, ch. 1182 (H.B. 3453), eff. 2011-09-01; subsection (d) adds a 21 percent ceiling for certain no-merchant-discount accounts) [VERIFIED]
- **Am. Pearl Grp., L.L.C. v. Nat'l Payment Sys., L.L.C., No. 24-0759 (Tex. May 23, 2025) (Sullivan, J.)** · Answering a Fifth Circuit certified question, the Court held that when a loan provides for periodic principal payments, § 306.004(a)'s "actuarial method" requires computing maximum permissible interest on the declining principal balance for each payment period, rejecting the "equal parts" method (total principal multiplied by rate by years) urged under Nevels and Tanner, which the Court distinguished as interest-only-loan cases rather than overruled. "When a loan provides for periodic principal payments, Section 306.004(a)'s mandate to use the 'actuarial method' requires courts to calculate maximum permissible interest based on the declining principal balance for each payment period." https://search.txcourts.gov/SearchMedia.aspx?MediaVersionID=f22fe346-20be-4a11-9bea-2536c000c0b6&coa=cossup&DT=OPINION&MediaID=eda6225e-08c6-42f0-bd12-b5d40a97dd07 (verified 2026-09-16; slip opinion delivered 2025-05-23; docket shows rehearing dismissed 2025-07-11 and case stored 2025-08-05; the Fifth Circuit applied the answer in No. 23-10804, per curiam 2025-07-15, citing 2025 WL 1478179; § 306.004 unamended through 89th 2nd C.S., 2025; S.W.3d citation not located) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. Fin. Code § 304.003(a)-(c)** · Money judgments without a contract rate earn postjudgment interest at the Federal Reserve prime rate on the computation date, with a 5 percent floor and 15 percent cap, set monthly by the consumer credit commissioner. "(2) five percent a year if the prime rate as published by the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System described by Subdivision (1) is less than five percent; or" https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/finance-code/title-4/subtitle-a/chapter-304/subchapter-a/section-304-003/ (verified 2026-09-16; Justia 2025 Texas Statutes, identical to official text current through 89th 2nd C.S., 2025; last amended Acts 2005, ch. 387 (S.B. 1450) and ch. 1018 (H.B. 955), eff. 2005-09-01) [VERIFIED]

## Usury penalties, defenses, limitations

- **Tex. Fin. Code § 305.001(a)** · A creditor that contracts for, charges, or receives usurious interest in a personal, family, or household transaction owes the greater of three times the excess interest or the lesser of $2,000 or 20 percent of principal. "three times the amount computed by subtracting the amount of interest allowed by law from the total amount of interest contracted for, charged, or received; or (2) $2,000 or 20 percent of the amount of the principal, whichever is less." https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/finance-code/title-4/subtitle-a/chapter-305/subchapter-a/section-305-001/ (verified 2026-09-16; Justia 2025 Texas Statutes, identical to official text current through 89th 2nd C.S., 2025; last amended Acts 2005, ch. 1018 (H.B. 955), § 2.05, eff. 2005-09-01) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. Fin. Code § 305.001(a-1), (c)** · In a commercial transaction, liability attaches only to contracting for or receiving usurious interest (not merely charging), and equals three times the excess interest; overcharges within the lawful maximum are not usury penalties. "A creditor who contracts for or receives interest that is greater than the amount authorized by this subtitle in connection with a commercial transaction is liable to the obligor for an amount that is equal to three times the amount" https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/finance-code/title-4/subtitle-a/chapter-305/subchapter-a/section-305-001/ (verified 2026-09-16; same fetches; (a-1) reads "contracts for or receives" and omits "charges"; (c) is general, not limited to commercial transactions; eff. 2005-09-01) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. Fin. Code § 305.002(a)-(b)** · Charging and receiving more than twice the authorized interest adds forfeiture of principal plus all interest and other amounts, but only in personal, family, or household transactions, not commercial ones. "a creditor who charges and receives interest that is greater than twice the amount authorized by this subtitle is liable to the obligor for: (1) the principal amount on which the interest is charged and received" https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/finance-code/title-4/subtitle-a/chapter-305/subchapter-a/section-305-002/ (verified 2026-09-16; Justia 2025 Texas Statutes, identical to official text current through 89th 2nd C.S., 2025; last amended Acts 2005, ch. 1018, § 2.06, eff. 2005-09-01) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. Fin. Code § 305.006(a)** · A usury action must be filed within four years after the usurious interest was contracted for, charged, or received, in one of five enumerated counties (transaction, charge, creditor residence or principal office, obligor residence). "An action under this chapter must be brought within four years after the date on which the usurious interest was contracted for, charged, or received." https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/finance-code/title-4/subtitle-a/chapter-305/subchapter-a/section-305-006/ (verified 2026-09-16; Justia 2025 Texas Statutes, identical to official text current through 89th 2nd C.S., 2025; last amended Acts 2005, ch. 1018, § 2.07, eff. 2005-09-01) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. Fin. Code § 305.006(b)-(d)** · An obligor must give the creditor written notice stating in reasonable detail the nature and amount of the violation not later than the 61st day before filing suit for usury penalties; the creditor may cure under § 305.103 from receipt of the notice through the 60th day after and is then not liable; on a usury counterclaim the defendant gives the same notice at filing and, on the creditor's application, the court abates the action for 60 days from its order, during which the creditor may cure and must offer the obligor's reasonable attorney's fees. "Not later than the 61st day before the date an obligor files a suit seeking penalties for a transaction in which a creditor has contracted for, charged, or received usurious interest, the obligor shall give the creditor written notice" https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/finance-code/title-4/subtitle-a/chapter-305/subchapter-a/section-305-006/ (verified 2026-09-16; same fetches; abatement under (d) is not automatic, it arises on application of the creditor to the court; eff. 2005-09-01) [VERIFIED]

"A creditor is not subject to penalty under this chapter for any usurious interest that results from an accidental and bona fide error." https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/finance-code/title-4/subtitle-a/chapter-305/subchapter-b/section-305-101/ (fetched 2026-09-16; Justia 2025 Texas Statutes; eff. 1999-09-01) [CONTEXT: not independently verified this pass]

## Commercial loans: qualified commercial loans and equity kickers

- **Tex. Fin. Code § 306.001(9)** · A "qualified commercial loan" is a commercial loan of $3 million or more if secured by real property, or $250,000 or more if unsecured by real property; below $500,000 the documents must carry the borrower's certification that it was advised to and could consult an attorney and accountant; renewals qualify regardless of balance. "(a) $3 million or more if the commercial loan is secured by real property; or (b) $250,000 or more if the commercial loan is not secured by real property" https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/finance-code/title-4/subtitle-a/chapter-306/subchapter-a/section-306-001/ (verified 2026-09-16; Justia 2025 Texas Statutes, matched against official FI.306 current through 89th 2nd C.S., 2025; last amended Acts 2021, ch. 276 (H.B. 3514), eff. 2021-09-01; (9)(B) excludes TxDMV-licensed dealer financing) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. Fin. Code § 306.101(a)-(c)** · Parties to a qualified commercial loan may contract up to the applicable ceiling and may also agree to underwriting discounts, conversion rights, options to buy equity, and profit or revenue participations in the obligor or its affiliates; those charges are not interest. "(c) A charge under Subsection (b) is not interest." https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/finance-code/title-4/subtitle-a/chapter-306/subchapter-b/section-306-101/ (verified 2026-09-16; Justia 2025 Texas Statutes, matched against official FI.306 current through 89th 2nd C.S., 2025; added Acts 1999, ch. 62, § 7.18(a), eff. 1999-09-01; no amendment since) [VERIFIED]

### Context (not independently verified)

- **Tex. Fin. Code § 306.103(a)-(b); § 306.001(1)** · A discount in an account purchase transaction (sale of accounts, instruments, documents, or chattel paper by a commercial enterprise) is not interest, and the parties' characterization as a purchase is conclusive for usury purposes even with a repurchase obligation. "the parties' characterization of an account purchase transaction as a purchase is conclusive that the account purchase transaction is not a transaction for the use, forbearance, or detention of money." https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/finance-code/title-4/subtitle-a/chapter-306/subchapter-b/section-306-103/ (fetched 2026-09-16; Justia 2025 Texas Statutes; eff. 1999-09-01; § 398.004 (2025) removes sales-based financing from this safe harbor, see Chapter 398 below) [CONTEXT: not independently verified this pass]

## Sponsor angles

Each angle rests only on VERIFIED bullets above. Posture: mechanism, authority, counterparty's best counter, what defeats it.

- **Commercial ceiling and computation.** Mechanism: price sponsor or portco commercial credit under a Chapter 303 ceiling election, capped at 28 percent, and test the schedule on the declining principal balance. Authority: § 303.009(c); American Pearl (Tex. 2025). Counter: the borrower recomputes a front-loaded or fee-stacked schedule on the declining balance and finds excess in the early periods. Defeats it: early-period interest above 28 percent of then-outstanding principal, or no ceiling election at all, which leaves the 10 percent default of § 302.001(b).
- **Commercial penalty exposure.** Mechanism: on a commercial transaction, liability attaches only to contracting for or receiving usurious interest, equals treble the excess, and principal forfeiture is unavailable. Authority: § 305.001(a-1), (c); § 305.002(b). Counter: the obligor recharacterizes the credit as personal, family, or household use to reach § 305.001(a) and § 305.002(a). Defeats it: a consumer purpose on the face of the documents or in the use of proceeds.
- **Cure window.** Mechanism: a usury notice opens a 60-day cure under § 305.103 that ends liability; on a counterclaim the creditor applies for a 60-day abatement and offers the obligor's reasonable attorney's fees. Authority: § 305.006(b)-(d). Counter: the obligor pleads usury as a counterclaim rather than a suit, or disputes that the cure tendered matches the violation noticed. Defeats it: letting the 60th day pass without a § 305.103 cure, or never applying for abatement.
- **Qualified commercial loan safe harbor.** Mechanism: size sponsor mezzanine or shareholder loans at $3 million or more (real-property-secured) or $250,000 or more (otherwise) and attach warrants, conversion rights, or profit participations as non-interest. Authority: § 306.001(9); § 306.101(a)-(c). Counter: the borrower argues the loan sits below the threshold or, under $500,000, lacks the attorney-and-accountant certification, so the kicker is interest tested against the ceiling. Defeats it: a missing certification on a sub-$500,000 loan or an original balance under the threshold; renewals qualify regardless of balance.
- **Limitations on the loan book.** Mechanism: usury claims against a target's loan book or legacy sponsor debt expire four years after the interest was contracted for, charged, or received, with venue limited to five counties. Authority: § 305.006(a). Counter: the obligor dates accrual from the most recent charge or receipt rather than from contracting. Defeats it: interest charged or received within the four years before suit.
- **Chapter 398 compliance as a portco provider or broker.** Mechanism: deliver the § 398.051(a) disclosures on every sub-$1 million offer to a Texas recipient, obtain the § 398.052 signature before finalizing, and register with OCCC through NMLS ($1,000 initial and annual fee) by 2026-12-31 if in business on 2025-09-01. Authority: H.B. 700 history; §§ 398.051(a), .052, .053; H.B. 700 § 2(a); 7 TAC §§ 86.301-.307. Counter: OCCC treats an unregistered provider or an unsigned offer as a chapter violation; a recipient points to the missing signature to contest the contract. Defeats it: an offer finalized without the signed disclosure, or business for compensation after the transition deadline without registration.
- **Foreclosure runway on Texas collateral.** Mechanism: a sponsor lender reaches sale on the first Tuesday (first Wednesday when that Tuesday is January 1 or July 4) after 21 days' posted, filed, and certified-mail notice stating the earliest sale time; residential debtors get 20 days to cure first. Authority: Tex. Prop. Code § 51.002(a), (a-1), (b), (d). Counter: the debtor attacks the notice (certified mail, the 21-day count, the earliest-time statement) or a sale outside 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. Defeats it: a notice that omits the earliest sale time or misses the 21-day count, or a sale outside the statutory window.
- **Homestead lending.** Mechanism: a portco lending against Texas homesteads underwrites to 80 percent combined LTV against all homestead debt of record, takes non-recourse paper, forecloses only by court order, and closes no earlier than the 12th day after application or notice. Authority: Tex. Const. art. XVI, § 50(a)(6)(B), (C), (D), (M)(i). Counter: the borrower asserts a defect under one of the enumerated conditions. Defeats it: an LTV above 80 percent at closing, a recourse provision beyond actual fraud, a non-judicial sale, or a closing before the 12th day.
- **Money transmission by a portco.** Mechanism: payment, escrow, or marketplace flows that hold or move customer funds require a Department of Banking license under Chapter 152 unless an authorized-delegate, § 152.004, or commissioner-granted exemption applies. Authority: § 152.101(a)-(c), (e). Counter: the department reads advertising or holding out as engaging in money transmission as licensable conduct on its own. Defeats it: compensation for transmission plus holding out, with no exemption on the record.
- **Consumer lending licensure.** Mechanism: bank-partnership or fintech consumer lending into Texas by a portco needs an OCCC license unless the bank or Chapter 156/157 exemption applies to the party making, transacting, or negotiating the loan. Authority: § 342.051(a)-(c-1). Counter: OCCC characterizes a program with a nominal exempt lender as a device, subterfuge, or pretense under (b). Defeats it: an exemption that belongs only to the nominal party while the portco makes, transacts, or negotiates the loan.
- **Carrying cost of receivables and judgments.** Mechanism: unpaid trade receivables and intercompany balances with no stated rate carry 6 percent legal interest from the 30th day after the due date; Texas money judgments without a contract rate earn the prime rate with a 5 percent floor and 15 percent cap. Authority: § 302.002; § 304.003(a)-(c). Counter: the debtor points to agreed compensation constituting interest, which fixes the rate that amount produces. Defeats it: a stated rate or compensation in the documents.
- **Attachment at closing.** Mechanism: paper the three § 9.203(b) elements in the sponsor credit agreement (value given, debtor rights in the collateral, an authenticated security agreement describing the collateral) so the lien is enforceable against the borrower and third parties from closing; for a deposit account, control under § 9.104 pursuant to the security agreement satisfies the (b)(3) condition. Authority: § 9.203(a)-(b). Counter: the borrower or a trustee attacks the collateral description or shows the debtor never had rights in an affiliate's assets. Defeats it: a security agreement signed by the wrong entity, or collateral the debtor had no rights in.
- **UCC-1 first, then fund.** Mechanism: file the Secretary of State financing statement against the portco before funding so the sponsor's priority date under § 9.322(a)(1) predates any competing filer and any lien creditor under § 9.317(a)(2)(B); a purchase-money lender has the 20-day window in § 9.317(e). Authority: § 9.310(a); § 9.322(a)(1)-(2); § 9.317(a)(2), (e). Counter: an earlier-filed blanket lienholder, or a judgment creditor whose lien arose before the filing, primes the sponsor. Defeats it: an earlier filing on the same collateral, or a gap between perfection and a lapse.
- **Cash collateral by control.** Mechanism: take the portco's operating accounts by deposit account control agreement (or lend through the depositary bank), because a financing statement never perfects a lien on a deposit account and perfection lasts only while control lasts; a springing agreement that leaves the borrower operating the account still confers control under § 9.104(b). Authority: § 9.312(b)(1); § 9.314(b); § 9.104(a)(1)-(3), (b). Counter: the bank terminates the agreement or the borrower moves the accounts, ending control and perfection together. Defeats it: lapse of the control agreement, or cash migrating to an uncovered account.
- **Priority over the depositary bank.** Mechanism: a control agreement beats every non-control party but not the depositary bank's own security interest; to prime the bank the sponsor lender either becomes the bank's customer on the account under § 9.104(a)(3) or negotiates the bank's subordination inside the control agreement. Authority: § 9.327(1)-(4). Counter: the bank asserts its § 9.327(3) priority against swept cash. Defeats it: no subordination and no customer status on the account.
- **Article 9 foreclosure and credit bid.** Mechanism: on default, repossess without judicial process where no breach of the peace occurs and demand assembly of the collateral, then dispose of it commercially reasonably; a sponsor lender that wants the collateral itself buys at a public disposition, since § 9.610(c)(2) bars a private-sale purchase of collateral not sold on a recognized market. Authority: § 9.609(b)-(c); § 9.610(a)-(c). Counter: the debtor or a guarantor attacks the method, manner, time, place, or terms as commercially unreasonable, or attacks the self-purchase at a private sale. Defeats it: a private-sale credit bid on portco equity or unique assets, or a disposition that fails commercial reasonableness on any element.

Authority: § 9.406(d). Defeats it: collateral inside one of the carve-outs, or an interest in a partnership or LLC, which § 9.406(j) removes from the section.

Authority: § 9.408(a), (d). Counter: the counterparty refuses performance to the lender, who cannot compel it or foreclose on the contract itself without consent. Defeats it: a value thesis that depends on operating the contract or license rather than on proceeds; for payment intangibles and promissory notes, (b) limits the override to security interests arising from a sale.

Defeats it: a filing in the state of operations rather than the state of organization; § 9.307(k) confines the rule to perfection and priority.

Authority: § 9.611(b)-(e). Counter: an omitted junior lienholder or guarantor attacks the sale and the deficiency. Defeats it: a search run outside the safe-harbor window that misses a filer, or a guarantor left off the list; the (d) exception covers only perishable, speedily declining, or recognized-market collateral.

Authority: § 9.612(a)-(b). Counter: the debtor shows the notice went out before default, or fewer than 10 days before the earliest stated time, and reasonableness becomes a fact question under (a). Defeats it: a notice sent before the default it relies on, or a disposition date inside the 10 days.
- **Deficiency after a noncompliant sale.** Mechanism: a sponsor lender pursuing a deficiency against the portco or its guarantors keeps proof of compliance with the disposition rules ready, because under § 9.626(a) once the debtor or a secondary obligor puts compliance in issue the lender bears the burden, and a failure caps the deficiency at the secured obligation, expenses, and fees less the greater of actual proceeds or what a complying sale would have realized, presumed to equal that full sum unless the lender proves less. Authority: § 9.626(a)(1)-(4). Counter: the guarantor pleads a defective notice or a commercially unreasonable sale and forces the lender to prove what a complying disposition would have fetched. Defeats it: no appraisal or market evidence of the complying-sale value, which leaves the presumption standing and the deficiency at zero; the § 9.628 exceptions were not fetched.
