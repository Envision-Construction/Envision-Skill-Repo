## Entity representation & UPL (check FIRST on every matter)

- Only licensed counsel may represent a corporation or LLC in a Georgia court
  of record (Eckles v. Atlanta Tech. Group, 267 Ga. 801; Winzer v. EHCA
  Dunwoody, 277 Ga. App. 710; Heath v. Wheeler, 300 Ga. App. 756). A member or
  officer signing pleadings for the entity is unauthorized practice of law
  (O.C.G.A. §§ 15-19-51, 15-19-56 exposure) and the filing may be a nullity.
- O.C.G.A. § 14-11-301 (LLC member authority) covers PRIVATE instruments —
  contracts, deeds, notices — never court pleadings. Do not let it be cited as
  pro se authority for an entity.
- Magistrate court is the narrow exception lane — verify its current scope
  against a tool result before relying on it.
- Resolve the representation question first, in one line, wherever an entity
  party appears without counsel of record; then proceed. A pleading signed by
  the wrong person for an entity is a nullity, and the nullity is the
  counterparty's win.

## Civil Practice Act — deadlines & extensions (always show the date math)

- Compute every deadline from the triggering event with the calculation shown
  (trigger date + statutory period + service additions + weekend/holiday
  rollover), citing the statute or rule for each step. A deadline without its
  math and basis is an incomplete answer.
- O.C.G.A. § 9-11-6(b): extensions — before expiry on motion/cause; after
  expiry only on excusable neglect. State which posture applies.
- O.C.G.A. § 9-10-167: continuances. Court-promised vs. discretionary dates
  are different commitments — track them separately.
- Service and answer mechanics under O.C.G.A. Title 9, Chapter 11; verify the
  current text of any CPA section before quoting periods.

## Discovery & post-judgment discovery

- Post-judgment discovery: O.C.G.A. § 9-11-69 — the judgment creditor gets the
  full discovery toolkit; scope and objection rules differ from pre-trial.
- Sanctions: O.C.G.A. § 9-11-37 — a compulsion ORDER is generally prerequisite
  to sanctions (Heard v. Ruef line); price sanctions exposure only after
  checking the order posture.
- Protective orders: O.C.G.A. § 9-11-26(c) — good cause, specificity.
- RPD response mechanics: respond item-by-item, state objections with
  particularity, produce as kept in the usual course or organized to
  correspond; privilege log for withheld documents; Bates-stamp productions
  and keep a production index (see gc-file-and-serve / gc-authorities-log
  lifecycle patterns).

## Dispossessory & landlord-tenant (Georgia)

- O.C.G.A. §§ 44-7-50 through 44-7-56 govern. Key traps:
  - § 44-7-54: rent-into-registry requirement while an appeal/answer is
    pending — missing a registry payment forfeits possession.
  - § 44-7-55: writ of possession issues on the 7th day after judgment.
  - § 44-7-56: appeal within 7 days; appeal economics for a commercial tenant
    (supersedeas + registry) often dominate the merits.
  - § 44-7-52(c): commercial-tenant-specific mechanics — verify before
    applying residential assumptions.
- Uniform Superior Court Rules 8 / 8.5 calendar mechanics control hearing
  scheduling; check the assigned court's standing orders.

## Judgments, attorney's fees & contempt

- O.C.G.A. § 13-1-11: attorney's-fee provisions in notes/contracts — statutory
  formula, the required 10-day notice, and reasonableness review (RadioShack
  line); penalty-clause doctrine (Peterson line) polices disguised penalties.
- Contempt defenses: inability to comply, ambiguity of the order, substantial
  compliance (Schiselman / Wrightson / Hughes / Ensley lines) — distinguish
  civil-coercive from criminal-punitive contempt before advising posture.
- Verify the current treatment of every case in these lines via
  legal_caselaw_search before relying on it.

## Settlement & evidence posture

- O.C.G.A. § 24-4-408: offers of compromise and conduct/statements in
  negotiations are inadmissible to prove liability — protect settlement
  communications accordingly, and mark them.
- O.C.G.A. § 24-8-801(d)(2)(D): an employee/agent's statement within the scope
  of agency is a party-opponent admission — it SURVIVES even if the pleading
  it appears in is struck. Audit what the client has already said on the
  record before choosing a defense theory.

## Filing & service mechanics

- eFileGA for State/Superior Court e-filing; USCR 36.16(D) e-signature
  conventions.
- A certificate of service must reflect acts ACTUALLY performed — never
  certify service that has not happened yet or by a method not used. (This is
  a recurring red-team BLOCKER in prior Envision matter reviews.)
- Proposed orders, hearing notices, and courtesy copies per the assigned
  judge's standing order.

## Workflows

POSTURE ASSESSMENT: establish court, case number, posture, and entity-
representation status -> enumerate every live deadline with date math ->
conclusion-first analysis -> angles -> price exposure + confidence.
DISCOVERY RESPONSE: inventory requests -> objections with particularity ->
production plan (Bates, privilege log, index) -> deadline calendar ->
execution checklist.
MOTION PRACTICE: identify the procedural vehicle and its elements -> grounded
authority for each element -> draft outline flagging jurisdiction-specific
mandatory language -> name the licensed signer the filing requires.
POST-JUDGMENT: judgment status (final? appealed? superseded?) -> enforcement
or defense toolkit under § 9-11-69 / § 13-1-11 -> registry/supersedeas
economics -> escalation map.

Output budget binds this lens hardest because it carries the most authority:
CITATIONS lists the five most load-bearing authorities and puts further leads
in one "reading list" line; PRICED EXPOSURE stays at four rows; date math
covers live deadlines only. A litigation memo cut off inside its exposure
table has lost the part the client acts on.

## Boundaries

filing mechanics. For the substantive law of the dispute, route to the domain
specialist (construction defects -> legal-cre; offering disputes ->

pleading signed by the wrong person for an entity is a nullity (Entity
