## FAA text and Supreme Court doctrine (by year)

### Statutory text (9 U.S.C.)

- **9 U.S.C. § 2** · A written arbitration provision in a contract evidencing a transaction involving commerce is valid, irrevocable, and enforceable except on generally applicable contract-law grounds and, since the 2022 amendment, except as chapter 4 (EFAA) provides. "shall be valid, irrevocable, and enforceable, save upon such grounds as exist at law or in equity for the revocation of any contract or as otherwise provided in chapter 4." https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title9-section2&num=0&edition=prelim (verified 2026-09-16; OLRC text of laws in effect Sept. 15, 2026; July 30, 1947, 61 Stat. 670; chapter-4 clause inserted by Pub. L. 117-90, § 2(b)(1)(A), Mar. 3, 2022, applying to disputes arising or accruing on or after that date; no later amendment) [VERIFIED]
- **9 U.S.C. § 1; New Prime Inc. v. Oliveira, No. 17-340 (U.S. Jan. 15, 2019), slip op. 3-6** · The FAA does not apply to contracts of employment of seamen, railroad employees, or any other class of workers engaged in foreign or interstate commerce, and whether that exclusion applies is for the court, not the arbitrator, before arbitration is ordered under §§ 3-4, a delegation clause notwithstanding. "nothing herein contained shall apply to contracts of employment of seamen, railroad employees, or any other class of workers engaged in foreign or interstate commerce." https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title9-section1&num=0&edition=prelim and https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/18pdf/17-340_o7kq.pdf (verified 2026-09-16; OLRC text of laws in effect Sept. 15, 2026, source credit July 30, 1947 only; New Prime slip opinion, Gorsuch, J., Kavanaugh, J., not participating; the exemption was widened, not narrowed, by Flowers Foods, Inc. v. Brock, No. 24-935 (U.S. May 28, 2026), in Context below) [VERIFIED]
- **9 U.S.C. § 9** · Where the parties agreed that judgment may be entered on the award, any party may apply within one year after the award is made to the court specified in the agreement (or, absent one, the district where the award was made) for confirmation, which the court must grant unless the award is vacated, modified, or corrected under §§ 10-11. "at any time within one year after the award is made any party to the arbitration may apply to the court so specified for an order confirming the award, and thereupon the court must grant such an order" https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title9-section9&num=0&edition=prelim (verified 2026-09-16; OLRC text of laws in effect Sept. 15, 2026; July 30, 1947, 61 Stat. 672; no amendments; Jules v. Andre Balazs Properties, No. 25-83 (U.S. May 14, 2026), addressed jurisdiction over § 9 motions in stayed actions and left the text untouched) [VERIFIED]
- **9 U.S.C. § 10(a)(1)-(4)** · A district court may vacate an award only for corruption, fraud, or undue means; evident partiality or corruption; arbitrator misconduct prejudicing a party; or arbitrators exceeding their powers or failing to make a mutual, final, and definite award. "(4) where the arbitrators exceeded their powers, or so imperfectly executed them that a mutual, final, and definite award upon the subject matter submitted was not made." https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title9-section10&num=0&edition=prelim (verified 2026-09-16; OLRC text of laws in effect Sept. 15, 2026; subsections restructured by Pub. L. 107-169, May 7, 2002, after Pub. L. 101-552 (1990) and 102-354 (1992); nothing later; "only" reflects the enumerated structure, not a statutory word; § 10(c) adds a separate ground for 5 U.S.C. § 580 administrative awards outside this pinpoint) [VERIFIED]
- **9 U.S.C. § 12** · Notice of a motion to vacate, modify, or correct an award must be served on the adverse party within three months after the award is filed or delivered. "Notice of a motion to vacate, modify, or correct an award must be served upon the adverse party or his attorney within three months after the award is filed or delivered." https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title9-section12&num=0&edition=prelim (verified 2026-09-16; OLRC text of laws in effect Sept. 15, 2026; July 30, 1947, 61 Stat. 673, derived from Act of Feb. 12, 1925, ch. 213, § 12; no amendments) [VERIFIED]
- **9 U.S.C. § 16(a)-(b)** · Orders refusing a § 3 stay, denying a § 4 petition, confirming or denying confirmation, or vacating, modifying, or correcting an award are immediately appealable, as are final decisions; interlocutory orders granting a stay or compelling arbitration are not appealable except under 28 U.S.C. § 1292(b). "(a) An appeal may be taken from- (1) an order- (A) refusing a stay of any action under section 3 of this title, (B) denying a petition under section 4 of this title to order arbitration to proceed," https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title9-section16&num=0&edition=prelim (verified 2026-09-16; OLRC text of laws in effect Sept. 15, 2026; added as § 15 by Pub. L. 100-702, § 1019(a), Nov. 19, 1988; renumbered § 16 by Pub. L. 101-650, Dec. 1, 1990; nothing later; OLRC renders the dash as a hyphen) [VERIFIED]
- **9 U.S.C. § 402(a); Pub. L. 117-90, § 3 (effective-date note at § 401)** · At the election of a person alleging a sexual harassment or sexual assault dispute (or a class or collective representative), no predispute arbitration agreement or predispute joint-action waiver is valid or enforceable as to a case filed under federal, tribal, or state law relating to that dispute. "no predispute arbitration agreement or predispute joint-action waiver shall be valid or enforceable with respect to a case which is filed under Federal, Tribal, or State law and relates to the sexual assault dispute or the sexual harassment dispute." https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title9-section402&num=0&edition=prelim (verified 2026-09-16; OLRC text of laws in effect Sept. 15, 2026; added by Pub. L. 117-90, § 2(a), Mar. 3, 2022, 136 Stat. 27; § 3 note, 136 Stat. 28, applies the chapter to disputes or claims arising or accruing on or after Mar. 3, 2022; no amendment, and searches found no injunction or constitutional challenge) [VERIFIED]

### Supreme Court doctrine, by year (stay, waiver, appeal, jurisdiction)

- **Morgan v. Sundance, Inc., 596 U.S. 411, 417 (2022), No. 21-328 (U.S. May 23, 2022)** · Federal courts may not create arbitration-specific procedural rules; waiver of the right to arbitrate through litigation conduct does not require a showing of prejudice to the opponent. "For that reason, the Eighth Circuit was wrong to condition a waiver of the right to arbitrate on a showing of prejudice." https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/21pdf/596us1r33_a86c.pdf (verified 2026-09-16; preliminary print; unanimous, Kagan, J.; the Court assumed without deciding that federal waiver law governs; Jules (608 U.S. 200, 2026) cites it approvingly at 413, 418-419; operative) [VERIFIED]
- **Badgerow v. Walters, 596 U.S. 1 (2022), No. 20-1143 (U.S. Mar. 31, 2022)** · Vaden's look-through approach applies only to § 4 petitions; a federal court assessing jurisdiction over an application to confirm or vacate looks only at the application itself. "We hold it does not. Those sections lack Section 4’s distinctive language directing a look-through, on which Vaden rested. Without that statutory instruction, a court may look only to the application actually submitted to it in assessing its jurisdiction." https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/21pdf/596us1r20_f2ag.pdf (verified 2026-09-16; preliminary print, Page Proof Pending Publication; 8-1, Kagan, J., Breyer dissenting) [UNVERIFIED-CURRENCY: not overruled, but Jules v. Andre Balazs Properties, 608 U.S. 200 (May 14, 2026), confines it to freestanding §§ 9-10 applications; the reach of its state-court conclusion where a § 3 stay is in place was not established this pass]
- **Coinbase, Inc. v. Bielski, 599 U.S. 736, 740 (2023), No. 22-105 (U.S. June 23, 2023)** · When a party takes a § 16(a) interlocutory appeal from denial of a motion to compel arbitration, the district court must stay its proceedings while the appeal is pending, under the Griggs divestiture principle. "The sole question here is whether the district court must stay its pre-trial and trial proceedings while the interlocutory appeal is ongoing. The answer is yes: The district court must stay its proceedings." https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/22pdf/599us1r48_986b.pdf (verified 2026-09-16; preliminary print; 5-4, Kavanaugh, J., Jackson, J., dissenting, joined by Sotomayor, Kagan, and Thomas in part; Spizzirri (601 U.S. 472) and Jules (608 U.S. 200) cite it as governing; CourtListener shows no negative treatment) [VERIFIED]
- **Smith v. Spizzirri, 601 U.S. 472, 478-479 (2024), No. 22-1218 (U.S. May 16, 2024)** · When a court finds a dispute arbitrable and a party requests a stay, § 3 compels a stay and the court lacks discretion to dismiss; dismissal would trigger the immediate appeal Congress barred in § 16(b). "When a district court finds that a lawsuit involves an arbitrable dispute, and a party requests a stay pending arbitration, §3 of the FAA compels the court to stay the proceeding." https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/23pdf/601us2r22_o7jq.pdf (verified 2026-09-16; preliminary print; unanimous, Sotomayor, J.; the resolved circuit split is catalogued at n. 1; Jules relies on it at 474, 476-478; operative) [VERIFIED]
- **Jules v. Andre Balazs Properties, 608 U.S. 200 (2026), No. 25-83 (U.S. May 14, 2026)** · A federal court that stayed claims under § 3 in a case within its original jurisdiction retains jurisdiction to confirm or vacate the resulting award under §§ 9-10 without an independent jurisdictional basis on the face of those motions; Badgerow governs only freestanding applications. "it retains jurisdiction to determine whether the arbitral award resolving those claims is valid and should be confirmed. The judgment of the Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit is therefore affirmed." https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/608us1r31_8759.pdf (verified 2026-09-16; preliminary print, Page Proof Pending Publication; unanimous, Sotomayor, J.; judgment issued June 15, 2026, no rehearing petition on the docket; resolved the split between CA2/CA3/CA7 (George, Kinsella) and CA4 (SmartSky), not CA9 as the finder first recorded) [VERIFIED]

[CONTEXT: not independently verified this pass]

## Delegation, non-signatories, class and mass arbitration

- **First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan, 514 U.S. 938, 944 (1995), No. 94-560 (U.S. May 22, 1995)** · Who decides arbitrability turns on what the parties agreed; courts apply ordinary state contract law but will not find an agreement to arbitrate arbitrability absent clear and unmistakable evidence, and review de novo where none exists. "Courts should not assume that the parties agreed to arbitrate arbitrability unless there is "clea[r] and unmistakabl[e]" evidence that they did so." https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/94-560.ZO.html (verified 2026-09-16; LII mirror of the slip opinion, supremecourt.gov holds pre-1991 opinions only in bound volumes; Breyer, J., unanimous; pinpoint 944 confirmed through Rent-A-Center n. 1 and Coinbase v. Suski (602 U.S. 143, 2024), which quotes the sentence as governing; operative) [VERIFIED]
- **Rent-A-Center, West, Inc. v. Jackson, 561 U.S. 63, 70-72 (2010), No. 09-497 (U.S. June 21, 2010)** · A delegation provision is an additional, antecedent arbitration agreement enforceable under § 2; unless a party challenges the delegation provision specifically, the court enforces it and leaves challenges to the agreement as a whole for the arbitrator. "unless Jackson challenged the delegation provision specifically, we must treat it as valid under §2, and must enforce it under §§3 and 4, leaving any challenge to the validity of the Agreement as a whole for the arbitrator." https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/09-497.ZO.html (verified 2026-09-16; LII mirror, the supremecourt.gov slip PDF returns 404; Scalia, J., 5-4, Stevens, J., dissenting; Suski (2024) cites 561 U.S. at 67, 70-72 as governing and glosses severability without overruling; operative) [VERIFIED]
- **AT&T Mobility LLC v. Concepcion, 563 U.S. 333 (2011), No. 09-893 (U.S. Apr. 27, 2011)** · The FAA preempts California's Discover Bank rule classifying most collective-arbitration waivers in consumer adhesion contracts as unconscionable, because requiring classwide arbitration interferes with fundamental attributes of arbitration. "Requiring the availability of classwide arbitration interferes with fundamental attributes of arbitration and thus creates a scheme inconsistent with the FAA." https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/09-893.ZO.html (verified 2026-09-16; LII mirror, the supremecourt.gov slip PDF returns 404; Scalia, J., 5-4, Breyer, J., dissenting; reporter cite confirmed through the CourtListener citation resolver; 23 Supreme Court opinions apply it, none overruling; OT2025 arbitration decisions do not reach it; operative) [VERIFIED]
- **Epic Systems Corp. v. Lewis, 584 U.S. 497 (2018), No. 16-285 (U.S. May 21, 2018)** · The FAA requires enforcement of employment arbitration agreements providing for individualized proceedings; neither the § 2 saving clause nor the NLRA's concerted-activity right displaces that command. Two fragments split at the original's em dash: "In the Federal Arbitration Act, Congress has instructed federal courts to enforce arbitration agreements according to their terms" and "including terms providing for individualized proceedings." https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/17pdf/16-285_q8l1.pdf (verified 2026-09-16; slip opinion p. 2; 5-4, Gorsuch, J., Ginsburg, J., dissenting; 584 U.S. 497 confirmed from the bound-volume table of cases; no reversal located, and CRS Legal Sidebar LSB11442 (June 25, 2026) describes class-waiver enforceability as settled; the only statutory carve-out is 9 U.S.C. ch. 4 (2022)) [VERIFIED]
- **Henry Schein, Inc. v. Archer & White Sales, Inc., 586 U.S. 63 (2019), No. 17-1272 (U.S. Jan. 8, 2019), slip op. 5** · When a contract delegates arbitrability to the arbitrator, a court may not decide it even if the argument for arbitration is wholly groundless; the court still decides whether a valid arbitration agreement exists. "When the parties’ contract delegates the arbitrability question to an arbitrator, a court may not override the contract. In those circumstances, a court possesses no power to decide the arbitrability issue." https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/18pdf/17-1272_7l48.pdf (verified 2026-09-16; slip opinion; unanimous, Kavanaugh, J.; the Court left open whether incorporating AAA rules delegated arbitrability; on remand the Fifth Circuit (No. 16-41674, Aug. 14, 2019) found no clear and unmistakable delegation; the second petition, No. 19-963, was granted June 15, 2020, argued Dec. 8, 2020, and dismissed as improvidently granted Jan. 25, 2021; holding intact) [VERIFIED]
- **Coinbase, Inc. v. Suski, 602 U.S. 143, 145 (2024), No. 23-3 (U.S. May 23, 2024)** · Where two contracts conflict, one delegating arbitrability and a later one sending disputes to court, a court decides which contract governs; the severability principle does not require the challenge to target only the delegation clause. Two fragments split at the original's em dash: "before either the delegation provision or the forum selection clause can be enforced, a court needs to decide what the parties have agreed to" and "i.e., which contract controls." https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/23pdf/602us1r26_k537.pdf (verified 2026-09-16; preliminary print; unanimous, Jackson, J., Gorsuch, J., concurring; severability discussion at 150-152; CourtListener shows no later Supreme Court treatment; operative) [VERIFIED]

### Context (not independently verified)

## Post-award review (FAA and TAA): exclusive grounds, expanded review by agreement, evident partiality

### FAA: Hall Street and Oxford Health

- **Hall Street Associates, L.L.C. v. ## Sponsor angles

Each angle rests only on VERIFIED bullets in this file; where a counter or a defeat is CONTEXT, it is marked.

- **Delegation as forum lock.** Mechanism: an express delegation sentence sends scope, validity, and public-policy attacks to the arbitrator; the court keeps only formation and an attack aimed at the delegation sentence itself. Authority: Rent-A-Center; Henry Schein (no wholly-groundless exception); RSL Funding; TotalEnergies (AAA Commercial Rules delegate between signatories, carve-outs do not defeat it); Cerna (duration is scope). Counterparty's best counter: attack the delegation clause specifically (Rent-A-Center) or plead prohibitive cost to arbitrate the threshold issue. What defeats it: Rafiei requires evidence of the arbitral cost of the threshold issue, the court cost, and inability to afford one but not the other; a bare affidavit fails. Draft the delegation as its own sentence rather than relying on rules incorporation alone, since Henry Schein left rules incorporation open and Jody James (CONTEXT) denies it against nonsignatories.
- **Appeal and freeze.** Mechanism: denial of a motion to compel is immediately appealable and the case stops for the appeal. Authority: § 16(a)(1)(B); Bielski; Texas § 171.098(a)(1). Counter: the plaintiff calls the appeal dilatory and asks to proceed. Defeats: Bielski leaves the district court no discretion under Griggs. The asymmetry cuts the other way too: an order compelling arbitration is not appealable (§ 16(b); § 171.098(a) omits it), so a counterparty that loses the motion waits for the award.
- **Stay, never dismiss.** Mechanism: when compelling arbitration in federal court, request a § 3 stay; the stayed case supplies jurisdiction to confirm or vacate later and blocks the plaintiff's immediate appeal. Authority: Spizzirri; Jules; § 16(b). Counter: the counterparty cites Badgerow to force confirmation into state court. Defeats: Jules confines Badgerow to freestanding applications.
- **Waiver clocks.** Mechanism: under the FAA a counterparty that litigates before demanding arbitration forfeits the forum with no showing of prejudice; the sponsor's own demand goes in the first responsive filing. Authority: Morgan. Counter: in Texas state court the counterparty invokes Perry Homes (prejudice required). Defeats: Morgan bars arbitration-specific procedural rules for FAA-governed agreements; the Texas Supreme Court has not resolved the conflict and the courts of appeals split (recorded in the Perry Homes currency note), so the Morgan argument is live in every Texas FAA case.
- **Post-award asymmetry.** Mechanism: the loser's window to attack is short (§ 12; § 171.088(b); DRAA § 5809(b)) and the winner's window to confirm is long with confirmation mandatory absent a timely ground (§ 9; § 171.087; DRAA § 5810(a)). Counter: the loser disputes the delivery or filing date that starts the clock. Defeats: serve the award by a method that fixes the date, and read the day-count from the statute on the day it matters, never from this file.
- **Bilateral-only programs.** Mechanism: class and collective waivers in employment and consumer agreements are enforceable and state rules conditioning enforcement on class procedures are preempted. Authority: Epic Systems; Concepcion; § 2. Counter: the claimant elects out under EFAA § 402(a) for a sexual-harassment or sexual-assault dispute, or claims the § 1 transportation-worker exemption. Defeats: nothing defeats a valid EFAA election (disputes arising or accruing on or after Mar. 3, 2022); the § 1 question belongs to the court before any order compelling (New Prime, with § 1), so the program is audited by job function before the motion is filed; the § 1 expansion line (Saxon, Bissonnette, Flowers Foods) is CONTEXT.
- **Nonsignatory capture in Texas.** Mechanism: wrongful-death beneficiaries, trust beneficiaries, and downstream purchasers are bound without signing. Authority: Labatt (derivative claims); Rachal (direct-benefits estoppel, no formal contract required); Whiteley (subsequent purchaser suing on the original agreement's warranties). Counter: the nonsignatory pleads in tort and disclaims the contract. Defeats: the estoppel turns on whether the claim seeks the contract's benefits; plead that dependence from the counterparty's own pleadings.
- **Choice-of-law drafting against the TAA exclusions.** Mechanism: a clause governed by the FAA sidesteps § 171.002(a)(2)-(3) (individual transactions of $50,000 or less; personal-injury claims) and the attorney-signature cure in (b)-(c). Authority: § 2; § 171.002(a)(2), (b), (c); Nexion's preemption holding stands, recorded above under a SUPERSEDED bracket that reaches only its review-path clause. Counter: the counterparty argues the clause chose the TAA alone (Olshan, CONTEXT) or that no interstate commerce is shown. Defeats: name the FAA in the clause and plead the commerce facts.
- **Delaware fast track.** Mechanism: a DRAA award lands in 120 days (extensions capped at 60 days in aggregate), the challenge window is 15 days in the Delaware Supreme Court on FAA grounds, and the award is deemed confirmed on the fifth business day after the window. Authority: §§ 5808(b)-(c), 5806(b), 5809(a)-(d), 5810(a). Counter: the counterparty disputes eligibility under § 5803(a) (CONTEXT: signed agreement, Delaware entity or principal place of business, no consumer, Delaware law, express DRAA reference). Defeats: draft every eligibility element into the clause and elect at signing whether to waive review entirely under § 5809(d).
- **Business court routing.** Mechanism: from Sept. 1, 2025 a qualifying commercial award is confirmed or vacated before the business court, which HB 40 wrote into TAA venue and procedure without adding jurisdiction. Authority: § 171.002(d); HB 40. Counter: the counterparty contests the business court's own jurisdictional thresholds (Gov't Code § 25A.004(d-1), VERIFIED in the business-court jurisdiction section above; the threshold-arithmetic angle below works them) or moves to transfer under § 171.097 (CONTEXT). Defeats: plead the venue facts § 171.096(e) requires (CONTEXT) together with the jurisdictional facts from the current Government Code text pulled that day.
- **California invoice discipline (binds the sponsor).** Mechanism: a portfolio employer that misses an arbitration invoice by 30 days past due is in default and the employee takes the case to court with tolling and sanctions. Authority: §§ 1281.97(a)(1), 1281.98(a)(1); Hohenshelt. Counter: the sponsor pleads FAA preemption. Defeats: Hohenshelt holds no preemption; the surviving structure is a contractual payment period longer than "due upon receipt" plus a Hohenshelt motion for relief on any non-willful miss.
- **Formalities in GA, SC, and AL, and the FAA bypass.** Mechanism: Georgia employment and residential real-estate clauses need all-signatory initials; South Carolina contracts need the first-page capitalized notice; Alabama denies specific enforcement outright. Authority: § 9-9-2(c)(9); § 15-48-10(a); § 8-1-41(3); § 2 supplies the bypass for a transaction involving commerce (Allied-Bruce, CONTEXT, is the Alabama application). Counter: the counterparty denies interstate commerce. Defeats: plead the commerce nexus; where the record cannot carry it, satisfy the state formality at signing (initials; first-page notice).
- **Layered clauses.** Mechanism: where an LPA arbitrates and a later side letter or SPA selects a court, a court decides which contract controls. Authority: Suski. Counter: the counterparty relies on the later forum clause to keep the fight in court. Defeats: a precedence clause naming the arbitration agreement as controlling across the document set removes the conflict Suski hands to the court.

- **Expanded review, Texas route only.** Mechanism: an FAA §§ 9-11 proceeding cannot enforce a clause adding court review for legal error (Hall Street: the statutory grounds are exclusive), but a Texas-law clause that caps the arbitrator's authority so that reversible error is exceeding powers under § 171.088(a)(3)(A) buys appellate-style review of a large award in Texas state court, and the FAA does not preempt it (Nafta Traders, Parts II and III). Authority: Hall Street; Nafta Traders, 339 S.W.3d at 97, 101; §§ 171.087, 171.088(a)-(c). Counter: the counterparty gets the confirmation fight into federal court, where Hall Street governs, or argues the clause is not a clear agreement to expanded review. Defeats: draft the limitation expressly as a cap on the arbitrator's authority, name the TAA and Texas courts, and run the arbitration on a record with preserved objections (the clear-agreement and record requirements are CONTEXT, Nafta Traders Part IV); the fallback that survives in either forum is review inside arbitration, which the DRAA authorizes by statute at § 5809 (appellate arbitration, VERIFIED above).
- **Post-award attack ceiling.** Mechanism: a loser attacking an award on a contract-construction theory meets § 10(a)(4) read as construed-at-all, not construed-correctly (Oxford Health), and in Texas state court § 171.088 supplies the exclusive grounds with common-law manifest disregard gone (Hoskins). Authority: Oxford Health; Hoskins; § 10(a)(1)-(4); §§ 171.087, 171.088(a)-(c). Counter: the loser pleads manifest disregard or serious legal error. Defeats: in a TAA proceeding Hoskins ends the argument; under the FAA the only reversible award is one where the arbitrator abandoned the contract, so a sponsor holding the award budgets for confirmation (§ 9; § 171.087) rather than a merits appeal, and a sponsor on the losing side has the § 12 or § 171.088(b) window and nothing more unless it pre-agreed a Nafta Traders limitation.
- **Disclosure audit as award insurance.** Mechanism: in Texas the evident-partiality rule is disclosure-based and strict: a neutral's failure to disclose facts that might create a reasonable impression of partiality to an objective observer vacates the award without proof of actual bias (TUCO), a partial disclosure does not cure it, and a conflicts waiver predicated on full disclosure waives nothing the party never learned (Tenaska). Authority: TUCO, 960 S.W.2d at 630, 636; Tenaska; § 10(a)(1)-(4) (evident partiality at (a)(2)); § 171.088(a)(2)(A), recorded in the §§ 171.087-.088 bullet. Counter: the award-defender argues the undisclosed relationship was trivial (TUCO at 637, CONTEXT) or, in federal court in the Fifth Circuit, that vacatur needs a significant compromising relationship (Positive Software, CONTEXT). Defeats: on the defending side, serve every arbitrator a written disclosure questionnaire before the first hearing and renew it whenever the panel or the parties' counsel changes; on the attacking side, investigate the neutral's undisclosed dealings with opposing counsel's firm and choose the forum, since the same nondisclosure that fails in the Northern District of Texas under Positive Software (CONTEXT) can succeed in Texas state court under TUCO and Tenaska.
- **Business court threshold arithmetic.** Mechanism: from Sept. 1, 2025 a confirmation or vacatur application can be filed in the business court only if a claim in the arbitrated controversy is one described by Gov't Code § 25A.004(b) or (d), which carry the $5 million amount-in-controversy floor computed without interest, statutory or exemplary damages, penalties, fees, and costs, with no floor where a party is a publicly traded company under (c). Authority: § 25A.004(d-1); § 171.002(d); HB 40. Counter: the counterparty pleads that the floor is not met, that the claim is an excluded insurance-policy, bodily-injury, legal-malpractice, or consumer-transaction claim, or moves to transfer under § 171.097 (CONTEXT). Defeats: plead the amount-in-controversy and party facts from the current § 25A.004 text on the day of filing together with the division venue facts § 171.096(e) requires (CONTEXT); for a fund-level or portfolio-company award above the floor the business court is open and the appeal runs to the Fifteenth Court of Appeals (§ 25A.007(a), recorded in the § 25A.004(d-1) currency note); below the floor, or on an excluded claim, the application stays in district court.
- **Cross-border award routing (chapter 2).** Mechanism: an award against a non-U.S. counterparty, or between U.S. entities where the relationship involves property abroad, performance or enforcement abroad, or another reasonable relation with a foreign state, falls under the Convention (§ 202); the sponsor has three years, not one, to confirm, and the court shall confirm unless a Convention ground for refusal or deferral is found (§ 207); in the Fifth Circuit the resisting party bears the burden and the defenses are construed narrowly (Karaha Bodas). Authority: § 202; § 207; Karaha Bodas. Defeats: plead the § 202 foreign-relation facts from the deal documents (situs of property, place of performance) and file in a district where the debtor has contacts or assets; federal jurisdiction under § 203 and the chapter 1 doctrines carried through § 208 are CONTEXT.
- **Federal disclosure floor.** Mechanism: Commonwealth Coatings makes a neutral's nondisclosure of a repeated and significant business relationship a § 10(a)(2) vacatur ground without proof of actual bias, so a sponsor on either side of an FAA award in federal court pins its disclosure demand to that opinion rather than to TUCO. Authority: Commonwealth Coatings, 393 U.S. at 149; § 10(a)(1)-(4). Counter: the award-defender invokes the White concurrence (advance disclosure, or a trivial relationship; CONTEXT) and Positive Software's significant-compromising-relationship gloss (CONTEXT). Defeats: the written disclosure questionnaire from the disclosure-audit angle above, served before the first hearing and renewed on any change of panel or counsel, turns every later-discovered relationship into a nondisclosure fact; on the attacking side the relationship must be shown repeated and significant, not a single engagement.
- **Perry Homes factor audit.** Mechanism: before a portfolio company answers a Texas suit, run the Perry Homes factors (knowledge of the clause, discovery volume, who initiated it, merits versus arbitrability, usefulness in arbitration, merits judgment sought) against its own conduct and demand arbitration in the first filing. Authority: Perry Homes, Part IV; Wesley. Counter: a counterparty that litigated first argues the sponsor's own discovery waived the clause. Defeats: in Fort Worth and El Paso the party asserting waiver must still prove prejudice (Wesley, following Complete Solar, CONTEXT), so a sponsor resisting a late demand pleads prejudice with a discovery ledger; Complete Solar's account of Dallas dropping the element (CONTEXT) means a Dallas-venued sponsor files its own demand first; Wesley is not yet final (mandate status setting Oct. 15, 2026).
- **Severability sentence as program insurance.** Mechanism: a Texas court severs an unconscionable term and enforces the rest where the term is not the essential purpose of the agreement and the agreement carries a severability clause, so a remedy cap in a portfolio company's employment or vendor clause does not sink the arbitration agreement. Authority: Poly-America, Part VI. Counter: the claimant argues the stripped remedy was the clause's reason for being, so the whole agreement fails; Venture Cotton (CONTEXT) is the FAA-side severance authority the counterparty will distinguish. - **Business court removal clock.** Mechanism: a sponsor sued in district court on a qualifying arbitration application moves the fight to the business court by a Rule 355 notice filed in both courts that pleads the jurisdictional and division-venue facts and attaches the record; agreed removal at any time, contested removal within 30 days of discovering the jurisdictional facts.
