## Pleadings, early dismissal, and the expedited track (TRCP 47, 91a, 169)

- **Tex. R. Civ. P. 91a.3(a)-(c), 91a.4** · A Rule 91a motion to dismiss a baseless cause of action must be filed within 60 days after the first pleading containing the challenged cause of action is served and at least 21 days before the hearing, must be granted or denied within 45 days after filing, and any response is due 7 days before the hearing (91a.4). "A motion to dismiss must be: (a) filed within 60 days after the first pleading containing the challenged cause of action is served on the movant; (b) filed at least 21 days before the motion is heard" https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1462349/texas-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf (verified 2026-09-16; txcourts.gov compilation last amended July 1, 2026; no Supreme Court rules order through Sept. 4, 2026 amends Rule 91a) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. R. Civ. P. 169(a)-(b)** · The expedited actions process applies when all claimants other than counter-claimants plead only monetary relief aggregating $250,000 or less, and a party prosecuting under the rule may never recover a judgment above $250,000, excluding interest, statutory or punitive damages, penalties, attorney fees, and costs. "In no event may a party who prosecutes a suit under this rule recover a judgment in excess of $250,000, excluding interest, statutory or punitive damages and penalties, and attorney fees and costs." https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1462349/texas-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf (verified 2026-09-16; July 1, 2026 compilation still reads $250,000; Misc. Docket No. 26-9037 (May 29, 2026) is preliminary approval only of a $325,000 figure, comments closed Sept. 1, 2026, expected effective Oct. 1, 2026, no final order on the orders list through Sept. 4, 2026; Gov't Code § 22.004(h-1) already reads $325,000) [VERIFIED]

## Summary judgment (TRCP 166a, rewritten effective March 1, 2026)

- **Misc. Docket No. 26-9012, Final Approval of Amendments to Rule 166a of the Texas Rules of Civil Procedure (Tex. Feb. 27, 2026), paras. 2-3; Tex. R. Civ. P. 166a(a), (h)(3)** · The Supreme Court rewrote Rule 166a in full effective March 1, 2026, for motions filed on or after that date; paragraph (a) defines traditional and no-evidence motions and paragraph (h)(3) requires the court to grant a no-evidence motion unless the respondent produces summary judgment evidence raising a genuine issue of material fact. The former no-evidence paragraph (i) is now (h)(3); (i) now governs the written ruling. "The amendments apply only to a motion for summary judgment filed on or after March 1, 2026." https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1462277/269012.pdf (verified 2026-09-16; effective March 1, 2026; implements S.B. 293 (ch. 1130) and H.B. 16 (89th Leg., 2d C.S., ch. 7); the 2026 comment states the rewrite changes no substantive law other than deadlines; the July 1, 2026 compilation carries identical text and no later 2026 order amends 166a) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. R. Civ. P. 166a(d)(1), (e)(1), (g)(1), (i) (as adopted by Misc. Docket No. 26-9012); Tex. Gov't Code § 23.303** · Under rewritten Rule 166a the nonmovant responds within 21 days after the motion is filed, the movant may reply within 7 days after the response, the hearing or submission may not be set within 35 days of filing and must be set within 60 days (90 for docket conditions, good cause, or movant agreement), and the court signs a written ruling within 90 days after the hearing or submission date; § 23.303 imposes the same 60/90-day setting and 90-day ruling requirements on business, district, and statutory county courts. "Except on leave of court or agreement of the parties, the nonmovant must file a response within 21 days after the motion is filed." https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1462277/269012.pdf (verified 2026-09-16; effective March 1, 2026; § 23.303 read at statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/GV/htm/GV.23.htm, added by S.B. 293 eff. ## Texas Citizens Participation Act (Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code ch. 27)

- **Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code §§ 27.003(b)-(c), 27.004(a)-(c), 27.005(a)** · A TCPA motion to dismiss must be filed not later than the 60th day after service of the legal action (extendable by agreement or for good cause); on filing, all discovery is suspended until the court rules, except limited discovery the court allows under § 27.006(b); the hearing must occur within 60 days of service of the motion (outer limits 90 or 120 days) and the ruling within 30 days after the hearing. "A motion to dismiss a legal action under this section must be filed not later than the 60th day after the date of service of the legal action." https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/CP/htm/CP.27.htm (verified 2026-09-16; official text current through the 89th Leg., 2nd C.S., 2025; § 27.003 last amended by H.B. 2730, eff. Sept. 1, 2019) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code §§ 27.005(b)-(d), 27.009(a)(1)-(2)** · Once the movant shows the legal action is based on or in response to protected speech, petition, or association, the court must dismiss unless the claimant establishes by clear and specific evidence a prima facie case for each essential element; on dismissal the court shall award the movant costs and reasonable attorney's fees and may award deterrent sanctions; § 27.009(b) permits fees against a movant whose motion is frivolous or solely for delay. "if the court orders dismissal of a legal action under this chapter, the court: (1) shall award to the moving party court costs and reasonable attorney's fees incurred in defending against the legal action" https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/CP/htm/CP.27.htm (verified 2026-09-16; current through 89th Leg., 2nd C.S., 2025; § 27.009 last amended 2019 (H.B. 2730); the statute prefixes the quoted clause with "Except as provided by Subsection (c)") [VERIFIED]

## Venue, limitations, and contractual limits on suit

- **Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 15.020(a)-(d)** · For a "major transaction" (a written agreement with aggregate stated consideration of at least $1 million, excluding personal, family, household, and personal-injury or wrongful-death transactions), suit must be brought in a county the defendant agreed to in writing and may not be brought in a county the plaintiff agreed to exclude, subject to unconscionability ((d)(1)), voidness under Business & Commerce Code ch. 272 ((d)(2)), and other-title venue statutes ((d)(3)). "\"major transaction\" means a transaction evidenced by a written agreement under which a person pays or receives, or is obligated to pay or entitled to receive, consideration with an aggregate stated value equal to or greater than $1 million." https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/CP/htm/CP.15.htm (verified 2026-09-16; amended by Acts 2025, 89th Leg., R.S., ch. 427 (H.B. 2960), § 3, eff. Sept. 1, 2025; site current through 89th Leg., 2nd C.S., 2025) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code §§ 16.003(a)-(b), 16.004(a), 16.051** · Suits for debt, fraud, breach of fiduciary duty, and specific performance of a real-property contract must be brought within four years of accrual; trespass, conversion, personal injury, and wrongful death within two years; any action without an express period, other than recovery of real property, within four years. "A person must bring suit on the following actions not later than four years after the day the cause of action accrues" https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/CP/htm/CP.16.htm (verified 2026-09-16; current through 89th Leg., 2nd C.S., 2025; § 16.004 last amended 1999, § 16.003 2005 (S.B. 15), § 16.051 unamended since 1985) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code §§ 16.070(a)-(b), 16.071(a)** · A contract term shortening the time to sue to less than two years is void, except in an agreement for the sale or purchase of a business entity where a party pays or receives consideration of at least $500,000; a contractual claim-notice condition requiring notice in less than 90 days is void. "A stipulation, contract, or agreement that establishes a limitations period that is shorter than two years is void in this state." https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-b/chapter-16/subchapter-d/section-16-070/ (verified 2026-09-16; inherited from ../../legal-contracts/references/texas.md, which fetched the primary source: its § 16.070(a), § 16.070(b), and § 16.071(a), (e), (f) bullets, the last at law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-b/chapter-16/subchapter-d/section-16-071/; this file read the same sections at statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/CP/htm/CP.16.htm, § 16.070 last amended 1991, site current through 89th Leg., 2nd C.S., 2025) [VERIFIED]

## Damages, fees, and responsibility

- **Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code §§ 41.003(a), (d); 41.008(b)-(c)** · Exemplary damages require clear and convincing proof of fraud, malice, or gross negligence and a unanimous jury, and are capped at the greater of two times economic damages plus noneconomic damages up to $750,000, or $200,000; § 41.008(c) removes the cap for listed felony conduct committed knowingly or intentionally, including Chapter 31 theft (third-degree felony or higher), forgery (§ 32.21), commercial bribery (§ 32.43), and misapplication of fiduciary property (§ 32.45); § 41.0115 gates net-worth discovery on a substantial-likelihood showing. "may not exceed an amount equal to the greater of: (1)(A) two times the amount of economic damages; plus (B) an amount equal to any noneconomic damages found by the jury, not to exceed $750,000; or (2) $200,000." https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/CP/htm/CP.41.htm (verified 2026-09-16; current through 89th Leg., 2nd C.S., 2025; § 41.008 last amended 2021 (H.B. 375, S.B. 109)) [VERIFIED]

### Context (not independently verified)

- **Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code §§ 33.001, 33.004(a), 33.013(a)-(b)** · In tort and DTPA actions a claimant more than 50 percent responsible recovers nothing; each liable defendant pays only its percentage unless its responsibility exceeds 50 percent (or it acted in concert with specific intent in listed felonies), which triggers joint and several liability; a defendant may designate a responsible third party by motion filed at least 60 days before trial, and § 33.004(l) permits a motion to strike the designation for no evidence after adequate discovery. "In an action to which this chapter applies, a claimant may not recover damages if his percentage of responsibility is greater than 50 percent." https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/CP/htm/CP.33.htm (fetched 2026-09-16; § 33.013 last amended 2021 (H.B. 375, S.B. 109); § 33.004 last amended 2011) [CONTEXT: not independently verified this pass]

## Interlocutory appeal, supersedeas, and arbitration appeals

- **Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 51.014(a)(7), (a)(12), (b), (d), (f)** · Interlocutory appeal lies from listed orders of district courts, county courts at law, statutory probate courts, county courts, or the business court, including special-appearance rulings ((a)(7)) and denial of a TCPA motion ((a)(12)); an appeal under (a)(3), (5), (8), or (12) stays all other trial-court proceedings; a permissive appeal under (d) requires an application to the court of appeals within 15 days after the order is signed ((f)). "An interlocutory appeal under Subsection (a)(3), (5), (8), or (12) also stays all other proceedings in the trial court pending resolution of that appeal." https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/CP/htm/CP.51.htm (verified 2026-09-16; amended by Acts 2025, 89th Leg., R.S., ch. 364 (H.B. 45), § 1, and ch. 912 (H.B. 40), § 9, both eff. - **Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 52.006(a)-(c)** · Security to suspend a money judgment on appeal equals compensatory damages plus interest and costs, may not exceed the lesser of 50 percent of the judgment debtor's net worth or $25 million, and the trial court must reduce it on a showing of substantial economic harm. "Notwithstanding any other law or rule of court, when a judgment is for money, the amount of security must not exceed the lesser of: (1) 50 percent of the judgment debtor's net worth; or (2) $25 million." https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/CP/htm/CP.52.htm (verified 2026-09-16; added by Acts 2003, 78th Leg., ch. 204, § 7.02, eff. ## Texas Business Court (Gov't Code ch. 25A)

- **Tex. Gov't Code § 25A.004(b)-(d), (i)** · The business court has concurrent jurisdiction over governance, derivative, securities, fiduciary, and Business Organizations Code actions exceeding $5 million ((b)(1)-(7)), over the same actions regardless of amount when a publicly traded company is a party ((c)), and over qualified transactions, contracts consenting to business-court jurisdiction, and IP or trade-secret actions exceeding $5 million ((d)(1)-(5)); the amount in controversy is the total of all joined parties' claims ((i)); (g)-(h) exclude governmental, estate, family, insurance, lien-foreclosure, personal-injury, legal-malpractice, and consumer claims. "the business court has civil jurisdiction concurrent with district courts in the following actions, including actions in which a district court has exclusive jurisdiction, in which the amount in controversy exceeds $5 million," https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/GV/htm/GV.25A.htm (verified 2026-09-16; amended by Acts 2025, 89th Leg., R.S., ch. 912 (H.B. 40), § 45, eff. Sept. 1, 2025; "qualified transaction" in § 25A.001(14) is $5 million (H.B. 40 § 43); site current through 89th Leg., 2nd C.S., 2025) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. ## Sponsor angles

Each angle rests only on a VERIFIED bullet above. Mechanism, authority, the counterparty's best counter, and what defeats the counter.

1. **Rule 91a as the first response to a thin pleading.** Mechanism: a sponsor or portfolio company served with a cause of action that fails on its face moves within 60 days of service, with no evidence, and has a ruling within 45 days of filing. Authority: TRCP 91a.3(a)-(c). Counter: the claimant repleads before the hearing and the fight moves to the amended pleading. What defeats it: calendar the 60-day clock on receipt of every new pleading, and draft the motion to the elements the pleading omits rather than the facts it alleges, so the amendment has to add facts the claimant does not have.
2. **Plead over the Rule 169 cap.** Mechanism: a claimant that pleads $250,000 or less is confined to the expedited track and can never take a judgment above $250,000 exclusive of interest, statutory or punitive damages, penalties, fees, and costs. Authority: TRCP 169(a)-(b). Counter: the defendant argues the figure was inflated to escape the track. What defeats it: plead the actual damages model with a documented basis in the demand; re-pull the threshold at use, since Misc. Docket No. 26-9037 proposes $325,000 from October 1, 2026 and Gov't Code § 22.004(h-1) already reads that figure. Status of the pending order and the statutory driver: angle 19.
3. **Summary judgment under the 2026 rewrite.** Mechanism: the response clock runs 21 days from filing regardless of setting; a no-evidence motion under 166a(h)(3) forces the respondent to produce evidence on every challenged element; the court must set the matter within 60 or 90 days and sign a written ruling within 90 days after the hearing or submission, and § 23.303 binds the court to the same setting and ruling deadlines. Authority: TRCP 166a(d)(1), (g)(1), (h)(3), (i); Gov't Code § 23.303; Misc. Docket No. 26-9012. Counter: the respondent asks leave of court, or the movant's agreement, to extend the 21 days; the rule allows both. What defeats it: file once discovery on the challenged element has closed, so the leave request has no footing, and cite 166a(h)(3), not 166a(i), for the no-evidence standard.
4. **TCPA against speech-based claims.** Mechanism: defamation, disparagement, or interference claims built on communications draw a 60-day motion that suspends discovery, shifts fees to the movant on dismissal ("shall"), and on denial yields an interlocutory appeal that stays all other trial-court proceedings. Authority: CPRC §§ 27.003(b)-(c), 27.005(b)-(c), 27.009(a)(1); § 51.014(a)(12), (b). Counter: the claimant carries the clear-and-specific prima facie burden on each element, or argues the claim sits outside the Act; a movant whose motion is found frivolous or solely for delay pays fees under § 27.009(b). What defeats it: file within 60 days of service with a record tying each challenged claim to a communication, and bring the motion only where the communicative basis is on the face of the petition.
5. **Mandatory venue in deal documents of $1 million or more.** Mechanism: a written agreement with stated consideration at or above $1 million is a "major transaction," and suit must be brought in the county the defendant agreed to and may not be brought in a county the plaintiff agreed to exclude. Authority: CPRC § 15.020(a)-(c). Counter: the plaintiff argues unconscionability or voidness under Business & Commerce Code ch. 272 ((d)(1)-(2)). What defeats it: name a Texas county in the clause and state the consideration on the face of the agreement so the $1 million threshold is proven from the document itself. Enforcement of the clause by mandamus under § 15.0642: angle 15.
6. **Four-year outside date on post-closing claims.** Mechanism: fraud, fiduciary-duty, debt, and specific-performance claims against sellers or managers carry a four-year period; conversion and property damage two. Authority: CPRC §§ 16.003(a), 16.004(a), 16.051. Counter: the claimant argues a later accrual date. What defeats it: document when the claimant had the facts, in closing deliverables and post-closing correspondence, so accrual is fixed on paper.
7. **Exemplary damages cap, and the theft and fiduciary carve-out.** Mechanism: punitive exposure is capped at the greater of two times economic damages plus noneconomic damages up to $750,000, or $200,000, on clear-and-convincing proof and a unanimous jury, and net-worth discovery waits on a substantial-likelihood showing. Authority: CPRC §§ 41.003(a), (d), 41.008(b), 41.0115. Counter: the plaintiff pleads a § 41.008(c) predicate (misapplication of fiduciary property, theft, forgery, commercial bribery) to lift the cap. What defeats it: the (c) predicates require knowing or intentional conduct, so the record on intent decides whether the cap holds; build that record early.
8. **Interlocutory appeal for the out-of-state sponsor.** Mechanism: denial of a special appearance ((a)(7)) or of a TCPA motion ((a)(12)) is appealable at once, and the (a)(12) appeal stays all other trial-court proceedings. Authority: CPRC § 51.014(a)(7), (a)(12), (b). Counter: the plaintiff argues the ruling falls outside the enumerated list, or seeks a permissive appeal of its own under (d) within the 15-day window in (f). What defeats it: plead the special appearance and the TCPA motion so the order is squarely an (a)(7) or (a)(12) ruling; what an (a)(7) appeal stays was not read this pass (gap below), so pull § 51.014(b) in full before promising a stay on a special-appearance appeal. Round 2 read § 51.014(b) in full: an (a)(7) appeal stays the commencement of trial only, subject to (c); see angle 16.
9. **Supersedeas cap on appeal.** Mechanism: the bond to suspend a money judgment is compensatory damages, interest, and costs, capped at the lesser of 50 percent of the debtor's net worth or $25 million, and the trial court must reduce it on a showing of substantial economic harm. Authority: CPRC § 52.006(a)-(c). Counter: the judgment creditor contests the net-worth showing. What defeats it: contemporaneous, auditable net-worth evidence prepared before judgment; § 52.007 (2023) offers alternative security for debtors under $10 million in net worth.
10. **Business Court as the sponsor's forum.** Mechanism: governance, derivative, fiduciary, securities, and Business Organizations Code claims above $5 million, any such claim with a publicly traded party, a qualified transaction, or a contract consenting to business-court jurisdiction belong there concurrently; a sponsor sued in district court or a county court at law removes within 30 days of service or of discovering the jurisdictional facts, and removal waives neither venue nor personal-jurisdiction defenses. Authority: Gov't Code §§ 25A.004(b)-(d), (i), 25A.006(d), (f), (j). Counter: the opponent argues the amount in controversy is $5 million or under, an exclusion in (g)-(h) applies, or the action was commenced before September 1, 2024 (In re ETC Field Services bars unilateral removal). What defeats it: (i) aggregates all joined parties' claims; a consent clause in the deal documents triggers (d)(2); for legacy cases the only route is the agreed motion with court permission under § 25A.021 (Lone Star NGL), so price the consent into the settlement of any side issue. Removal-clock divergence between rule and statute: angle 21.
11. **Counsel before the answer date.** Mechanism: an LLC or fund cannot answer, plead, or appear in Texas district or county court through an officer or in-house non-lawyer. Authority: Kunstoplast, 937 S.W.2d at 456; TRCP 7. Counter: none of substance; the exposure is a default. What defeats it: engage Texas counsel on service, and treat the justice-court exception (Context bullet) as confined to that court.
12. **Fifteenth Court routing.** Mechanism: appeals from state-agency suits and business-court matters go to the Fifteenth Court; an ordinary civil appeal from a district court cannot be steered there and will be transferred to the regional court. Authority: Gov't Code § 22.220(d)-(e); Kelley v. Homminga; In re Dallas County. Counter: the appellee moves to transfer an inappropriately noticed appeal under Rule 27a, as in Kelley. What defeats it: notice the appeal to the regional court unless the notice includes a § 22.220(d) matter; the constitutional challenge to the court is closed by In re Dallas County.
13. **Temporary injunction to hold the status quo.** Mechanism: a sponsor moving to stop a competing sale, freeze a transfer, or enforce a restrictive covenant pending trial pleads and proves a cause of action, a probable right to the relief sought, and a probable, imminent, and irreparable injury in the interim; the remedy preserves the status quo and never issues as of right. Authority: Butnaru, 84 S.W.3d at 204; TRCP 683 (order contents). Counter: the respondent argues the injury is compensable in damages and so not irreparable, and attacks any order that omits reasons, incorporates the petition, or sets no trial date. What defeats it: put in evidence why money cannot restore the position (a unique asset, a control right, equity in a closely held company), and hand the court a proposed order that carries its own reasons, describes the restrained acts without reference to the petition, and sets the cause for trial.
14. **Dissolve the injunction or price the bond.** Mechanism: an enjoined sponsor attacks the order under Rule 683 for missing reasons, incorporation by reference, or no trial setting, and contests a nominal bond under Rule 684, which the court must fix before issuance and which is the enjoined party's security for sums and costs adjudged if the order is dissolved; the trial court's discretion on amount is subject to review. Authority: TRCP 683, 684; CPRC § 51.014(b) first sentence. Counter: the applicant cures the order by amendment and offers a larger bond. What defeats it: prove the value frozen at the hearing so the bond is set against a number, and plan for the (a)(4) appeal not staying the commencement of trial, so the merits keep moving while the appeal runs. Supreme Court authority for the void-and-dissolve attack: angle 22.
15. **Enforce the deal-document venue clause by mandamus.** Mechanism: when a counterparty sues outside the county a $1 million-plus agreement names, the sponsor moves to transfer under § 15.020 and, if the motion is denied, applies to the court of appeals for a writ of mandamus before the later of the 90th day before the date trial starts or the 10th day after it receives notice of the trial setting; no interlocutory appeal lies from the venue determination. Authority: CPRC §§ 15.020(a)-(c), 15.0642(1)-(2), 15.064(a). Counter: the plaintiff argues the application is late under § 15.0642 or that the clause fails under § 15.020(d). What defeats it: calendar the § 15.0642 window from the first notice of a trial setting, and build the transfer-motion record (the written agreement showing stated consideration of $1 million or more) so the mandamus petition carries its proof. Completes angle 5.
16. **Special-appearance appeal: trial stops, discovery does not.** Mechanism: an out-of-state sponsor whose special appearance is denied appeals under (a)(7); the appeal stays the commencement of trial and nothing else, and (c) conditions the stay on the special appearance having been filed and requested for submission or hearing by the scheduling-order date or the 180th day after the answer. Authority: CPRC § 51.014(a)(7), (b) first sentence, (c)(1)-(2). Counter: the plaintiff presses merits discovery during the appeal and argues the special appearance was set late, forfeiting the trial stay. What defeats it: file the special appearance with the first filing and request a hearing date in the same instrument, and ask the trial court separately for any discovery stay, since (b) supplies none for an (a)(7) appeal. Completes angle 8.
17. **Plead business-court authority on the face of the petition.** Mechanism: an original pleading in the business court must plead facts establishing the court's authority and venue in an operating division; a challenge to authority is due within 30 days of the movant's appearance; a court lacking authority transfers or dismisses without prejudice; and because no rule has yet been adopted under Gov't Code § 25A.0041, these Part III rules and mandamus govern jurisdictional fights. Authority: TRCP 352, 354(a), (c)(2), (d)(2); Gov't Code §§ 25A.004(b)-(d), (i), 25A.0041(a). Counter: the opponent moves within 30 days arguing the aggregate falls at or under $5 million or an exclusion in § 25A.004(g)-(h) applies. What defeats it: plead each joined party's claim so § 25A.004(i) aggregation shows on the pleading, name the operating division and the county, and hold the opponent to its 30-day window; re-check the Court's orders list at use for a § 25A.0041 rule.
18. **Removal geography.** Mechanism: removal under § 25A.006(d) is barred from a county outside an operating division ((e)), and five divisions sit as of this pass: First (Dallas), Third (Austin), Fourth (San Antonio), Eighth (Fort Worth), Eleventh (Houston); a suit filed in a county outside those divisions cannot be removed. Authority: Gov't Code § 25A.006(d)-(e); Business Court home page (operational divisions). Counter: the plaintiff files in a county outside an operating division to keep the sponsor out of the business court. What defeats it: a § 25A.004(d)(2) consent clause paired with a § 15.020 venue clause naming a county inside an operating division, so the deal document fixes both forum and county.

19. **Cite the statute, not the pending rule, for the $325,000 figure.** Mechanism: Gov't Code § 22.004(h-1) already fixes $325,000 as the amount-in-controversy ceiling for the county-court-at-law expedited-resolution rules the Legislature ordered, effective December 4, 2025; the Rule 47(c) and Rule 169 amendments that would carry that figure into rules of general application are preliminary only (Misc. Docket No. 26-9037), October 1, 2026 is stated there as an expectation the Court may change, and the 2026 orders list through 26-9073 (September 4, 2026) shows no finalizing order. Authority: Gov't Code § 22.004(h-1); Misc. Docket No. 26-9037, paras. 2-3; Supreme Court 2026 administrative-orders list. Counter: the opponent treats October 1, 2026 as operative and pleads, or objects to, a damages tier on the $325,000 figure before a final order takes effect. What defeats it: on and after October 1, 2026 re-pull the orders list the day of filing; until a final order issues and takes effect the operative rule figure is $250,000 (TRCP 169(a)-(b)), and a consult that quotes $325,000 cites § 22.004(h-1), which by its text reaches county courts at law only. Completes angle 2.
20. **Calendar the Rule 13 rewrite for the signature block.** Mechanism: Misc. Docket No. 26-9054 preliminarily approves a Rule 13 rewrite, TRAP 9 and 52 amendments, and the repeal of Rule 57, with comments closed September 1, 2026 and an October 1, 2026 effective date stated as an expectation the Court may change; through 26-9073 (September 4, 2026) no finalizing order appears, so current Rule 13 and Rule 57 govern every signature and every sanctions motion until one does, and every filing signed on or after the final order's effective date carries the rewritten rule. Authority: Misc. Docket No. 26-9054, paras. 2-4; Supreme Court 2026 administrative-orders list. Counter: opposing counsel measures a filing signed before the effective date against the rewritten rule, or the sponsor's own filing after that date is drafted to the old one. What defeats it: date-stamp the orders-list check in the file for every Texas filing signed on or after October 1, 2026, and until the final order posts, frame any sanctions record under current Rule 13 and Rule 57.
21. **Plead both removal dates.** Mechanism: Rule 355(c)(2)(A) runs the 30-day non-agreed removal clock from the date the removing party discovered or reasonably should have discovered the jurisdictional facts, with no service trigger and no "later of"; Gov't Code § 25A.006(f)(1), as amended by H.B. 40 effective September 1, 2025, runs it from the later of service of process and discovery; the clocks diverge whenever service postdates discovery, and an agreed notice may be filed at any time. Authority: TRCP 355(c)(2)(A)-(B) (Misc. Docket No. 24-9037); Gov't Code § 25A.006(f)(1)(A)-(B); TRCP 352 (Part III controls on conflict). Counter: the counterparty invokes Rule 355 and Rule 352's Part III-controls clause to hold the sponsor to the discovery date; the sponsor invokes the statute's later-of clause. What defeats it: file within 30 days of the earliest date any party can argue the sponsor knew the jurisdictional facts (for a sponsor served with a petition pleading a $5 million-plus governance claim, the service date), and plead both the service date and the discovery date in the notice of removal so the statutory clock is preserved if the rule's is contested; no opinion reconciling the two was located (gap below), and a missed non-agreed clock leaves removal to all parties' agreement. Completes angle 10.
22. **Attack a defective temporary injunction as void, not merely erroneous.** Mechanism: the Supreme Court holds Rules 683 and 684 mandatory, so a temporary injunction that omits the trial setting or the bond is subject to being declared void and dissolved; in InterFirst the Court itself declared the order void and ordered it dissolved rather than remanding to cure, and Qwest restated the rule in 2000. Authority: InterFirst Bank, 715 S.W.2d at 641; Qwest, 24 S.W.3d at 337; TRCP 683, 684. Counter: the applicant asks the trial court to amend the order to add the setting and fix the bond before the appeal is decided, or argues the omission is harmless. What defeats it: move to dissolve in the trial court and notice the (a)(4) appeal on the same day, so the record fixes the order as signed, and frame the relief as dissolution of a void order, the disposition InterFirst itself entered. Supplies the Supreme Court authority behind angle 14.
