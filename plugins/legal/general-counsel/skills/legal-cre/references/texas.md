## Anti-indemnity (Ins. Code ch. 151, subch. C)

- **Tex. Ins. Code §§ 151.101, 151.102 (Acts 2011, 82nd Leg., ch. 1292 (HB 2093), eff. Jan. 1, 2012)** · In a construction contract for a project where the indemnitor provides or procures insurance subject to ch. 151 or Title 10, a provision requiring the indemnitor to indemnify, hold harmless, or defend another party against claims caused by the indemnitee's own negligence, fault, statutory breach, or breach of contract is void and unenforceable as against public policy. § 151.103 excepts bodily injury or death of the indemnitor's employees; § 151.105 lists eleven exclusions (single-family house, townhouse, duplex, and related land development; municipal public works; others) and § 151.001(2) keeps single-family projects out of "construction project"; § 151.151 bars waiver by contract. ## Residential Construction Liability Act (Prop. Code ch. 27)

## Sponsor angles

Each angle rests only on a VERIFIED bullet above. Mechanism, authority, the counterparty's best counter, what defeats the counter.

- **Venue and law are fixed at the Texas site for post-September-2025 paper.** A sponsor's master agreement or subcontract selecting Delaware or New York law and forum for a Texas project is void if entered into or renewed on or after Sept. 1, 2025, and suit lies only in the site county (Bus. & Com. Code § 272.001(a)-(c), HB 2960 § 4). Counter: the counterparty argues the contract predates the effective date, or invokes FAA preemption for the arbitration-seat piece. Defeats: an evergreen or renewal clause exercised after Sept. 1, 2025 is a "renewal" under enrolled § 4; the FAA argument has no located Texas decision under the "void" text, and the venue rule in (c) operates on court actions regardless of how the arbitration question resolves (cross-reference arbitration.md).
- **A derivative claimant without the § 53.056 notice has no lien to defend.** An owner-side portfolio company treats a subcontractor lien as void where no (a-1)/(a-2) notice reached owner and original contractor by the 15th day of the third month after the furnishing month, and treats the asset as lien-clear the day after the § 53.052 filing window closes; any surviving lien dies for foreclosure one year after that last filing day (§§ 53.052, 53.056(a), 53.158(a)). Counter: the claimant reclassifies the project as non-residential to lengthen its window, or invokes the § 53.003(e) weekend rule. Defeats: the furnishing month and the notice date are matters of record, and § 53.056(a) conditions validity on the notice, so a claimant who cannot produce it loses before reaching the merits; the § 53.003(e) roll-forward is VERIFIED above and since May 21, 2025 reaches a fixed deadline as well as the last day of a computed period, so compute the notice and filing dates with the roll-forward before treating the window as closed.
- **An owner that released reserved funds early is a solvent defendant for an unpaid sub.** A subcontractor portfolio company on a project whose GC has failed recovers from the owner to the extent the owner paid out the 10 percent it was required to hold through the 30th day after completion (§§ 53.101(a), 53.105(a)). Counter: the owner shows the claimant missed a § 53.056 notice or the 30-day § 53.103 affidavit. Defeats: calendar discipline in the sub's AR function; the notice and affidavit dates are the entire case.
- **Broad-form indemnity in the counterparty's template is void and cannot be revived by waiver.** A contractor portfolio company facing an owner's or upstream GC's demand to defend and indemnify for the indemnitee's own negligence tenders only its proportional share (Ins. Code §§ 151.101-.102, § 151.151). Counter: the indemnitee invokes the § 151.103 employee-injury exception or a § 151.105 exclusion (single-family, municipal public works). Defeats: the exception reaches only bodily injury or death of the indemnitor's own employees, agents, or subcontractors, so property damage and third-party claims stay proportional; the exclusions are project-type facts fixed before signature, and S&B v. Scallon (CONTEXT above, not load-bearing here) shows the Court enforcing exactly the proportional structure this statute leaves standing.
- **Payment terms beyond 35 days are unenforceable against a GC portfolio company.** Statutory interest runs from day 36 on a written request for properly performed work, and § 28.006 voids any waiver of the chapter (Prop. Code § 28.002(a)). Counter: the owner disputes performance and withholds the disputed amount. Defeats: the dispute reaches only the amount withheld as authorized by statute; the undisputed balance still carries the clock.
- **By-right multifamily on commercial land in the big metros is an underwriting assumption, not an entitlement risk.** A developer portfolio company acquiring office, retail, or warehouse-zoned land in a city over 150,000 in a county over 300,000 builds multifamily at no less than 36 units per acre and 45 feet with one parking space per unit, on administrative approval (Loc. Gov't Code §§ 218.101-.102), and a rezoning that adds residential density survives neighbor protest on a simple majority unless 60 percent of adjoining owners sign (§ 211.0061(b), (d)); a five-acre single-family tract carries the 3,000-square-foot lot floor (§ 211.054). Counter: the city invokes a § 218.101(c) buffer (1,000 feet from heavy industrial, 3,000 feet from an airport or military base), argues the project was initiated before Sept. 1, 2025, or contests the population class. Defeats: site selection outside the buffers at LOI stage; a documented initiation date after Sept. 1, 2025; the § 218.301 civil action and the § 211.058 mandatory-fee remedy shift the cost of a wrong denial onto the city.
- **Plat approval runs on the applicant's clock.** A plat not disapproved with written, cited, non-arbitrary reasons by day 30 is approved by operation of law, and a second disapproval after the applicant's response is confined to the reasons first stated (Loc. Gov't Code §§ 212.009(a), 212.0091, 212.0095). Counter: the city requests a 30-day extension. Defeats: (b-2) allows extension only on the applicant's written request or for a takings impact assessment; decline in writing and the deemed-approval date holds.
- **A no-damages-for-delay clause does not reach owner interference.** A GC portfolio company keeps its delay claim, including against a local governmental owner, on proof the owner's conduct was arbitrary and capricious, active interference, bad faith, or fraud (Zachry, 449 S.W.3d 98). Counter: the owner characterizes the delay as ordinary or negligent and points to the negotiated clause. Defeats: the contemporaneous project record (directives, correspondence, schedule impacts) showing intent; the Court's public-policy holding applies to contract liability, so sophistication of the parties is not the answer; 89R SB 776 died, so the rule stands as of this pass.
- **Design error is the owner's problem in contract, not the architect's in tort.** A GC portfolio company's remedy for defective plans runs against the owner under its contract; an architecture-firm portfolio company sued by a GC in negligent misrepresentation moves for judgment under the economic loss rule (LAN/STV, 435 S.W.3d 234). Counter: the GC pleads privity or third-party-beneficiary status under the design contract. Defeats: no contract between GC and architect is the holding's predicate; a design contract that disclaims third-party beneficiaries keeps the shield intact.
- **A design-professional defendant gets an early exit without a conforming affidavit.** An architecture or engineering portfolio company sued without a same-discipline Texas-licensed affidavit filed with the first pleading moves to dismiss, presses for dismissal with prejudice, and takes the interlocutory appeal either way (CPRC § 150.002(a), (b), (e), (f)). Counter: the plaintiff invokes the (i) governmental design-build exemption, or amends with the certificate after a dismissal without prejudice while other defendants remain (Studio E, CONTEXT above). Defeats: the exemption is limited to third-party claims on governmental design-build projects; the with-prejudice request is the whole fight, and the immediate appeal is available on either outcome.
- **Project cash is trust money and the sweep is personal exposure.** A sponsor placing officers or directors at a contractor portfolio company holds them as trustees of every construction payment; moving $500 or more upstream before current or past-due beneficiary obligations are paid is a Class A misdemeanor and, with intent to defraud, a third-degree felony (Prop. Code §§ 162.002, 162.031(a), 162.032). Turned around, an unpaid subcontractor portfolio company holds the upstream GC's officers personally, which moves settlement. Counter: the trustee raises the § 162.031(b)-(c) affirmative defenses. Defeats: project-level segregated accounts and a payment ledger that shows beneficiary obligations paid first.
- **A public owner that skipped the payment bond stands as surety.** A subcontractor portfolio company unpaid on a public work contract over $25,000 (over $50,000 for a municipality or ch. 22 joint board) where the entity never obtained the payment bond looks to the entity itself (Gov't Code §§ 2253.021, 2253.027). Counter: the entity argues the contract fell under the threshold. Defeats: the contract amount is a public record.
- **The RCLA sequence caps homeowner recovery.** A homebuilder portfolio company that receives the 60-day notice inspects within 35 days and makes a written repair or settlement offer within 60 days; a claimant who rejects a reasonable offer is capped at the offer plus pre-rejection fees, and damages stay inside the § 27.004(g) categories (Prop. Code § 27.004(a), (b), (e), (g)). Counter: the claimant attacks the offer as unreasonable or untimely. Defeats: a dated written offer with scope, schedule, and cost support; § 27.009 bars waiver, so the sequence cannot be contracted away by either side.
- **A non-conforming lien release binds no one.** An owner-side portfolio company demands § 53.284-form releases, signed by the claimant, with evidence of payment behind every conditional release; a contractor portfolio company that signed a non-statutory release form keeps its lien (Prop. Code §§ 53.281, 53.284, 53.286). Counter: the owner pleads actual payment in full under § 53.282 or an accord and satisfaction under § 53.287. Defeats: the payment record; full payment is the only § 53.282 route on a commercial project, and the accord must post-date the dispute.
- **Deed fraud and sham lien filings against a Texas asset are felonies with a ten-year clock.** A sponsor holding Texas land or distressed title refers a fraudulent conveyance, a forged signature, or a sham lien recorded through the clerk to the district attorney under Penal Code §§ 31.23 and 32.60 (second-degree felony below $300,000 appraisal-roll value, first-degree at $300,000 or more; ten-year limitations under CCP art. 12.01(2)(J)), which changes the leverage in a lien-release or title-cure negotiation. Counter: the counterparty argues the conduct predates Dec. 4, 2025, or that the grade should follow the contract price. Defeats: SB 16 § 12 fixes the offense-date rule and § 31.23(f) fixes value by the appraisal roll, both matters of record; the restitution and recorded-judgment mechanics (CCP arts. 42.0376, ch. 5C) are CONTEXT above and not load-bearing here.
- **The in-person recording gate is a closing-logistics item, not a title risk.** A closing agent or runner who walks a deed to the county clerk must present photo identification or the conveyance will not record (Prop. Code § 12.001(b)(2); Loc. Gov't Code § 191.010(b)); a recorded instrument stays valid even if the clerk failed to copy the identification (§ 191.010(d)). Counter: a counterparty attacks a recorded deed because the clerk's ID copy is missing. Defeats: the (d) savings clause; electronic recording is outside § 191.010(b) on the statute's face, and whether an e-recording rule adds a gate is an open item in NOT VERIFIED THIS PASS.
- **Public-owner receivables run on a statutory clock with interest that cannot be contracted away.** A GC portfolio company's pay app to a city, school district, or special-purpose district is overdue on the 31st day (46th where the governing body meets monthly or less often) after the latest of delivery, completion, or invoice receipt; interest runs from the overdue date at one percent plus the prior-July prime rate, a waiver is void, and the prevailing party recovers fees (Gov't Code §§ 2251.021(a)-(b), 2251.025, 2251.004, 2251.043). Counter: the entity asserts a bona fide dispute and withholds. Defeats: the dispute notice must issue by the 21st day and withholding is capped at 110 percent of the disputed amount (§ 2251.042(a), (d)); the undisputed balance keeps the clock; the 60-day audit rule in § 2251.002(c) is CONTEXT above and not load-bearing here.
- **A claim against a local governmental owner lives or dies on the written contract.** The immunity waiver reaches only a properly executed written contract stating the essential terms for goods or services (Loc. Gov't Code § 271.152), contractual notice and ADR preconditions are enforceable (§ 271.154), and recovery is capped at the contract balance as amended plus owner-caused delay or acceleration cost, directed change orders or additional work, equitable and just fees, and ch. 2251 interest, with no consequential damages beyond (a)(1), no exemplary damages, and no unabsorbed home office overhead (§ 271.153(a)-(b)). Counter: the entity pleads immunity because the extra work rests on oral or unsigned change orders, or the contractor prices Eichleay-style overhead and lost profits on other work. Defeats: a signed change-order trail is the waiver itself; price the claim inside § 271.153(a) from the first demand and pair the (a)(1) delay category with Zachry's public-policy holding on owner interference.
- **Public retainage on a $5 million-plus job is five percent, and the subcontract cannot hold more than the owner does.** A GC portfolio company bidding Texas public work of $5 million or more prices retainage at five percent (10 percent under $5 million, and 10 percent on dam work regardless of value), and a subcontractor portfolio company strikes any retainage clause that exceeds the percentage the entity may withhold from the prime, because the cap flows down every tier (Gov't Code § 2252.032(b)(1)-(3), (d)). Counter: the entity or the prime argues the subchapter does not reach the contract. Defeats: § 2252.031(1) reaches the state, counties, cities, school districts, and any statutory public-works authority, so the exits are the three in § 2252.033 (a contract executed before August 31, 1981, an estimate under $400,000 at execution, or TxDOT work under Transp. Code ch. 223) and the (i) water-supplier carve-out, each a matter of record.
- **Public retainage is due at completion, warranty period or not.** A contractor portfolio company demands release of all retainage when the contract work is complete and treats an owner's continued hold through the warranty period, or a hold to force rework of designer-specified, properly installed manufactured goods or systems, as a § 2252.032(e) violation; on the final pay application the entity may hold only for a bona fide dispute over compliance with the express contract terms or a surety's refusal to consent, and must give the basis in writing (Gov't Code § 2252.032(e)-(h)). Counter: the entity labels a punch-list disagreement a bona fide dispute. Defeats: the written notice of the basis is mandatory, the dispute must be over express contract terms, and the prime's right to cure or to offer a reasonable sum for the noncompliant work puts the amount in issue at the noncompliant item; (h) preserves the contract remedies on both sides.
- **A Texas municipal abatement is 10 years on incremental value, and the chapter sunsets in 2029.** A development portfolio company models a ch. 312 abatement at no more than 10 years, on value above the base year fixed at execution, with existing improvements and pre-period personal property (inventory and supplies included) taxable in full, and expects every owner in the zone to get the same percentage and duration (Tax Code § 312.204(a)-(b)); the chapter expires September 1, 2029 unless continued, and both the 2027 and 2029 regular sessions precede that date (§ 312.006).
