## Texas Securities Act (Gov't Code title 12, chs. 4001-4008)

- **Tex. Gov't Code § 4004.051** · No dealer, person, or company may offer, sell, or make a sale of securities in Texas without first registering under chapter 4004, subject to the exemptions in § 4001.056(d) and chapter 4005, subchapter A. "may not, directly or through the dealer's or other person's or company's agents, offer for sale, sell, or make a sale of any securities in this state unless the dealer or other person or company is first registered" https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/government-code/title-12/chapter-4004/subchapter-b/section-4004-051/ (verified 2026-09-16; Justia 2025 Texas Statutes mirror and official text current through 89th Leg., 2nd C.S.; added by H.B. 4171, eff. Jan. 1, 2022, no later amendment; TSSB reports no 89th Legislature change to the Act) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. Gov't Code § 4005.012(a)** · Issuer sales made without public solicitation or advertisement are exempt where the issuer has no more than 35 security holders after the sale ((a)(1)) or sells to no more than 15 purchasers for their own account in the 12 months ending with the sale ((a)(2)); subsection (b) excludes certain purchasers from the (a)(2) count. The section applies the § 4005.001 exemption to these sales. "the sale of a security by the issuer of the security if the total number of security holders of the issuer does not exceed 35 persons after the sale" https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/government-code/title-12/chapter-4005/subchapter-a/section-4005-012/ (verified 2026-09-16; Justia 2025 mirror and official text current through 89th Leg., 2nd C.S.; H.B. 4171, eff. Jan. 1, 2022, no later amendment) [VERIFIED]
- **7 Tex. Admin. Code § 114.4(b)(1)** · For a Rule 506 offering the issuer provides the Securities Commissioner the Form D notice and the § 4006.052 fee when the Form D is filed with the SEC and no later than 15 days after the first sale of the federal covered securities in Texas, filed electronically through EFD ((b)(1)(A)-(C)); the notice stays effective until the offering is completed or terminated ((f)(5)). "at the time the Form D is filed with the SEC, but no later than 15 days after the first sale of the federal covered securities in this state, the issuer shall provide to the Securities Commissioner" https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/7-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-114-4 (verified 2026-09-16; Cornell LII mirror plus TSSB electronic rulebooks dated 2024-11-14 and 2026-03-26; last amended eff. Nov. 14, 2024, 49 TexReg 8874; TSSB adoptions of Mar. 13, 2025 and Mar. 26, 2026 and the Nov. 21, 2025 proposals reached only chs. 115, 116, 133) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. Gov't Code § 4006.052(a)-(b)** · The fee for the initial notice claiming a limited offering exemption is one-tenth of one percent of the aggregate amount of securities described as offered for sale, capped at $500. "shall charge and collect a fee of one-tenth of one percent of the aggregate amount of securities described as being offered for sale. (b) A fee charged under this section may not exceed $500." https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/government-code/title-12/chapter-4006/subchapter-b/section-4006-052/ (verified 2026-09-16; Justia 2025 mirror and official text current through 89th Leg., 2nd C.S.; H.B. 4171, eff. Jan. 1, 2022, no later amendment; indexed-figure rule applies: re-pull at use) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. Gov't Code § 4008.052(a)-(b)** · A person who offers or sells a security by means of an untrue statement of material fact or a misleading omission is liable to the buyer for rescission, or damages if the buyer no longer owns the security, whether or not the security or transaction was exempt; (c)-(d) supply defenses (Context bullet below). "is liable to the buyer of the security, regardless of whether the security or transaction is exempt under Chapter 4005, if the person offers or sells the security by means of an untrue statement of a material fact" https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/government-code/title-12/chapter-4008/subchapter-b/section-4008-052/ (verified 2026-09-16; Justia 2025 mirror and official text current through 89th Leg., 2nd C.S.; H.B. 4171, eff. Jan. 1, 2022, no later amendment to this section) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. Gov't Code § 4008.055(a)-(b)** · A person who directly or indirectly controls a seller, buyer, or issuer is jointly and severally liable to the same extent as the controlled party under §§ 4008.051-4008.054 unless the controlling person proves it did not know, and with reasonable care could not have known, the facts creating liability; the burden sits on the controlling person. "a person who directly or indirectly controls a seller, buyer, or issuer of a security is liable under Section 4008.051, 4008.052, 4008.053, or 4008.054 jointly and severally with the seller, buyer, or issuer and to the same extent" https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/government-code/title-12/chapter-4008/subchapter-b/section-4008-055/ (verified 2026-09-16; Justia 2025 mirror and official text current through 89th Leg., 2nd C.S.; H.B. 4171, eff. Jan. 1, 2022, no later amendment) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. Gov't Code § 4008.062(b)** · A § 4008.052 or § 4008.054 claim, and a related § 4008.055 claim, must be brought within three years of actual or reasonable-diligence discovery and in no event more than five years after the sale; (b)(3)-(4) add bars tied to a compliant rescission offer (30-day written rejection with reserved right to sue; one year after rejection). "(1) more than three years after the date of discovery of the untruth or omission, or after the date discovery should have been made by the exercise of reasonable diligence; (2) more than five years after the date of the sale" https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/government-code/title-12/chapter-4008/subchapter-b/section-4008-062/ (verified 2026-09-16; Justia 2025 mirror and official text current through 89th Leg., 2nd C.S.; H.B. 4171, eff. "(2) the offeror or seller did not know, and in the exercise of reasonable care could not have known, of the untruth or omission." https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/government-code/title-12/chapter-4008/subchapter-b/section-4008-052/ (fetched 2026-09-16; Justia 2025 mirror; H.B. 4171, eff. "A person who directly or indirectly with intent to deceive or defraud or with reckless disregard for the truth or the law materially aids a seller, buyer, or issuer of a security is liable" https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/government-code/title-12/chapter-4008/subchapter-b/section-4008-055/ (fetched 2026-09-16; Justia 2025 mirror; H.B. 4171, eff. ## SB 29 (2025) and the Business Organizations Code

- **Tex. Bus. Orgs. Code § 21.419(c)-(d), (f)** · For covered corporations, directors and officers are presumed to act in good faith, on an informed basis, in the corporation's interest, and in compliance with law; neither the corporation nor a shareholder has a claim against them unless the claimant rebuts a presumption and proves a breach involving fraud, intentional misconduct, an ultra vires act, or a knowing violation of law, pleaded with particularity. Coverage scope (exchange listing or an affirmative election) is the § 21.419(a) Context bullet below. "Neither a corporation nor any of the corporation's shareholders has a cause of action against a director or officer of the corporation as a result of any act or omission in the person's capacity as a director or officer unless" https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/business-organizations-code/title-2/chapter-21/subchapter-i/section-21-419/ (verified 2026-09-16; Justia 2025 mirror and official BOC ch. 21 current through 89th Leg., 2nd C.S.; added by Acts 2025, 89th Leg., R.S., ch. 21 (S.B. 29), § 11, eff. ## Texas Supreme Court 2025: In re UMTH General Services (entity-level advisory duties, derivative forum)

- **In re UMTH General Services, L.P., No. 24-0024 (Tex. ## Sponsor angles

Each angle rests only on a VERIFIED bullet above. Mechanism, authority, the counterparty's best counter, and what defeats the counter.

1. **Statutory liability shield for sponsor-designated directors.** Mechanism: a Texas portfolio corporation within § 21.419's coverage carries the four presumptions and the fraud / intentional-misconduct / ultra vires / knowing-violation gate with particularity pleading, which turns a fiduciary complaint into a fraud complaint. Authority: Tex. Bus. Orgs. Code § 21.419(c)-(d), (f). Counter: the claimant pleads intentional misconduct on information and belief and seeks books and records first. Defeat: particularity under (f) is the statute's own requirement; the coverage condition in § 21.419(a) (listing or affirmative election) is a Context bullet, so confirm it live and paper the election in the governing documents before the dispute exists.
2. **Three-percent derivative floor.** Mechanism: a listed portfolio company, or a § 21.419 elector with 500 or more shareholders, writes a beneficial-ownership minimum for derivative standing into its certificate or bylaws, up to three percent of outstanding shares. Authority: § 21.552(a)(3); applied in Gusinsky v. Reynolds (N.D. Tex. Mar. 17, 2026) per the docket report carried in that bullet, the order unfetched. Counter: the plaintiff aggregates holders or argues the bylaw was adopted after the challenged conduct. Defeat: adopt at reincorporation or IPO, before any claim accrues; watch the Fifth Circuit appeal noticed Apr. 15, 2026, the only live threat to the ruling.
3. **Exclusive Texas forum plus jury waiver in the charter.** Mechanism: governing documents require internal entity claims, derivative claims included, to be brought only in a named Texas court and waive jury trial; the waiver binds without signatures and reaches anyone who acquired equity after adoption. Authority: §§ 2.115(b), 2.116(b)-(d). Counter: a plaintiff pleads a federal securities claim and argues the forum clause cannot reach it, pointing to § 2.115's own "consistent with applicable state and federal jurisdictional requirements" limiter. Defeat: draft the clause to cover internal entity claims and accept that federal-question claims leak; pair the forum designation with the Business Court (angle 4) so the state-law core sits before a bench.
4. **Business Court as the sponsor's forum.** Mechanism: governance, derivative, fiduciary, and Texas Securities Act claims above $5 million, or any such claim involving a publicly traded party, belong in the Business Court; a sponsor sued in district court removes within 30 days of service or of discovering the jurisdictional facts. Authority: Gov't Code §§ 25A.004(b)-(c), 25A.006(f). Counter: the plaintiff pleads the amount in controversy at or under $5 million or times a temporary-injunction application to compress the window. Defeat: (f)(1)(B) restarts the clock on later discovery of jurisdictional facts, and (f)(2) runs the window from the ruling on the pending TI application; a listed party removes the floor entirely under (c).
5. **Control-person exposure, priced.** Mechanism: a sponsor that controls a portfolio issuer is jointly and severally liable for the issuer's untrue statements to the same extent as the issuer, with rescission or damages the measure; the sponsor's only exit is proving it did not know and with reasonable care could not have known. Authority: §§ 4008.052(a)-(b), 4008.055(a)-(b). Counter: the buyer argues the sponsor sat on the board, saw the PPM, and knew. Defeat: a contemporaneous diligence record on every offering writing (who reviewed what, when) is the reasonable-care proof; § 4008.062(b) closes the window three years from discovery and five years from sale, so tail exposure on a raise ends at the five-year mark whatever the buyer learned later.
6. **Exempt status is not a liability shield; repose is.** Mechanism: Rule 506 status leaves § 4008.052 rescission intact ("regardless of whether the security or transaction is exempt"), so the sponsor's defense is disclosure quality plus the five-year bar, never the exemption. Authority: §§ 4008.052(a), 4008.062(b). Counter: the buyer pleads discovery inside three years of a late-surfacing fact. Defeat: (b)(2) is absolute at five years from sale; the rescission-offer bars in (b)(3)-(4) give the issuer a statutory route to shorten the window further (the mechanics are the § 4008.062(a) Context bullet; confirm live before using them).
7. **Texas Form D notice at each fund closing.** Mechanism: every closing with a Texas investor starts a 15-day clock for the Form D notice and fee, filed through EFD; the fee is one-tenth of one percent capped at $500. Authority: 7 TAC § 114.4(b)(1); Gov't Code § 4006.052(a)-(b). Counter: none of substance; the cost is capped and the filing is administrative. Defeat: calendar the notice against the first Texas sale, not the SEC filing date, and re-pull the day count and fee at use (deadline-rule and indexed-figure classes).
8. **State fallback exemption for small Texas rounds.** Mechanism: a portfolio issuer selling without public solicitation to 35 or fewer total holders after the sale, or 15 or fewer own-account purchasers in twelve months, has a state exemption that does not depend on a Rule 506 notice. Authority: § 4005.012(a). Counter: the buyer counts holders across all classes and argues the 35-person ceiling was breached. Defeat: the quoted text counts "security holders of the issuer" after the sale with no residence limiter, so run the count on the full cap table before relying on (a)(1); (a)(2)'s twelve-month purchaser count is the alternative prong.
9. **Shareholder-proposal thresholds for a Texas-listed portfolio company.** Mechanism: a nationally listed corporation that opts in requires $1 million or three percent held six continuous months and a 67-percent solicitation before a proposal reaches the ballot; nominations are excluded. Authority: S.B. 1057 § 1, adding § 21.373(e)-(f). Counter: the proponent argues the federal proxy-rule process governs and the state threshold cannot displace it. Defeat: the statute is in force since Sept. 1, 2025 with no ruling against it found this pass; the opt-in is a governing-document term, so adopt it before proxy season and let the proponent bear the challenge.
10. **SB 2337 carries zero leverage against ISS today.** Mechanism: the proxy-advisor disclosure law is enjoined as to ISS by a text order that has never been reviewed on the merits, and the Fifth Circuit appeal was dismissed on the appellants' own motions. Authority: ISS v. Paxton text order Aug. 29, 2025; Glass, Lewis & Co. v. Paxton, No. 25-50771 (5th Cir. Nov. 25, 2025). Counter: the Attorney General argues the injunction is party-specific and other advisors remain covered. Defeat: nothing in the verified record shows enforcement against any advisor; a sponsor issuer's proxy-fight plan treats ch. 6A as inert against ISS until final judgment (discovery closes Oct. 20, 2026) and treats the private declaratory remedy (Context bullet) as untested.
11. **Minority co-investors have no oppression buyout in Texas.** Mechanism: no common-law oppression claim exists, the receivership statute yields only a rehabilitative receiver, and director duties run to the corporation, not to an individual holder; minority protection is therefore a contract term the sponsor prices, not a remedy the court supplies. Authority: Ritchie v. Rupe; In re Estate of Poe. Counter: in a closely held Texas parent the minority sues derivatively without demand and reaches subsidiary misconduct through double-derivative standing under Sneed v. Webre. Defeat: an affirmative § 21.419 election in the governing documents (coverage condition is Context; confirm live), the § 2.115 forum designation, and the § 2.116 jury waiver move the derivative fight to a bench in the sponsor's chosen Texas court under a fraud-level pleading standard.
12. **Adviser posture is a gate, not a form.** Mechanism: no sponsor management company advises a Texas client or a Texas-based fund without one of three postures under § 4004.052: state registration, a subch. G notice filing as an SEC-registered adviser, or a title 12 exemption; SEC status and Texas client count pick the door. Authority: Tex. Gov't Code § 4004.052. Counter: the TSSB reads "in this state" through 7 TAC § 116.1(a)(5), so advice a Texas LP receives while in Texas is rendered in Texas whatever the sponsor's address. Defeat: pick the door before the first Texas subscription and paper it; the exemption door (angles 14 and 15) is a filing or a client-count record, not an argument made after the fact.
13. **Notice filing for the SEC-registered sponsor.** Mechanism: an SEC-registered adviser with Texas clients or a Texas office notice-files rather than registers; the filing is effective on receipt for the rest of the calendar year and renews, and the Board must authorize the adviser once the form, consent, and fee arrive. Authority: §§ 4004.301-4004.304; 7 TAC § 116.1(b)(2)(A)(i), (C). Counter: the TSSB treats a lapsed renewal as an unregistered period. Defeat: § 4004.304 renewal on or before expiration is a calendar item and the IARD filing under § 116.1(b)(2)(C) is the record; 7 TAC § 139.23(d) (Context bullet) makes an SEC registrant ineligible for the private fund adviser exemption, so the notice filing is the only exempt path and is kept current.
14. **Private fund adviser exemption as the unregistered sponsor's home.** Mechanism: a sponsor advising only 3(c)(1) or 3(c)(7) funds files its SEC exempt-reporting-adviser reports through IARD with Texas designated, keeps itself and its advisory affiliates clear of the § 139.23(b)(2) disqualification list, and produces records on the commissioner's written request; that is the whole Texas registration posture. Authority: 7 TAC § 139.23(a)-(b), (f). Counter: the TSSB points to a bad-actor event at an advisory affiliate or an unanswered records request and treats the exemption as lost from that date. Defeat: (b)(3)(B) allows a good-cause waiver, so disclose and seek it before the event surfaces in an exam; a standing records-response protocol answers (f). The 3(c)(1) qualified-client and custody overlays in § 139.23(c) are a Context bullet; confirm them live for any co-investment vehicle outside the Form ADV private equity fund definition.
15. **De minimis: five Texas clients and no Texas office.** Mechanism: an out-of-state sponsor with no Texas place of business and fewer than six Texas-resident clients in the preceding 12 months needs no Texas registration or notice filing, and its representatives without a Texas place of business ride the same exemption. Authority: 7 TAC § 116.1(b)(2)(A)(iv), (B). Counter: the TSSB counts each Texas LP as a client, or treats a deal partner working from Texas as a place of business, and the sixth client or the Texas desk ends the exemption. Defeat: count clients per adviser-client relationship on a rolling 12-month basis and keep the count in the compliance calendar; when the count approaches six, the § 139.23 filing (angle 14) is the fallback that costs a form, not a registration.
16. **The agency tail, priced.** Mechanism: an unregistered or late-filed adviser posture, or a defective offering, carries an administrative fine after notice and hearing capped at the greater of $20,000 per violation or the gross economic benefit gained, plus up to $250,000 where a victim was 65 or older, aggregated with any § 4007.154 civil penalty; the proceeding must start within five years of the violation, and an agreed-order payment is not an admission in private litigation. Authority: Tex. Gov't Code § 4007.106(a)-(b), (d)-(e). Counter: the TSSB frames each Texas subscription as a separate violation and measures gross benefit by the management fees earned. Defeat: the gross-benefit prong is a ceiling, so the fee record on the Texas accounts bounds the exposure; take an agreed order under (d) so the fine cannot be pleaded as an admission by LPs; the five-year commencement limit in (e) ends the tail. The cease-and-desist and felony provisions (§§ 4007.101-4007.104, 4007.201-4007.203) are Context bullets in which registration status is a criminal element; confirm them live before treating any lapse as administrative only.
17. **Short-form squeeze-out at 90 percent.** Mechanism: a parent holding at least 90 percent of each class and series of a Texas subsidiary merges it out on a parent governing-authority resolution that states the terms and the consideration paid to each minority interest, with no subsidiary vote or board action. Authority: Tex. Bus. Orgs. Code § 10.006(a)-(b), (d)-(g), (i). Counter: the minority attacks the price, not the vote, through appraisal, which the market-out never removes for the subsidiary's holders under § 10.354(c). Defeat: price the minority off a contemporaneous valuation and reserve for appraisal; confirm the § 10.355 post-merger notice (Context bullet) and the § 10.006(i) exclusions (partnership subsidiaries, § 10.005(d)(1) provisions) live before the resolution is signed.
18. **Appraisal exposure on a Texas take-private.** Mechanism: all-cash consideration to holders of a Texas-listed target does not qualify for the market-out, because (b)(3) limits the shielded consideration to listed or 2,000-holder interests, cash for fractions, or a combination, so appraisal is live; a stock-for-stock merger between listed entities is shielded. Authority: § 10.354(a)-(c). Counter: the target argues the sponsor's parent stock is listed and the mixed consideration sits within (b)(3). Defeat: read (b)(3) against the actual consideration mix before signing; where cash dominates, model appraisal in the price and use the perfection steps in §§ 10.355-10.356 (Context bullets; confirm live) to shrink the class of perfected dissenters.
19. **Constitutional tax floor under a Texas listing.** Mechanism: no future Texas statute can impose an occupation tax on a registered securities market operator or a tax on a securities transaction it conducts; the franchise tax survives as a general business tax measured by business activity. Authority: Tex. Const. art. VIII, § 30(a)-(c). Counter: a future legislature recharacterizes a levy as a general business tax or a document-processing fee under (c). Defeat: (a)'s operator definition and (b)'s two prohibitions are fixed constitutional text keyed to January 1, 2025 and January 1, 2026 reference dates and yield only to a further constitutional amendment; the SB 1058 rebate exclusion (Context bullet) is the statutory companion and is confirmed live before it enters a listing analysis.
20. **Appraisal is a corporate-form risk, not an LLC or LP risk.** Mechanism: subch. H reaches only a domestic for-profit corporation, professional corporation, professional association, or REIT; a Texas LLC or LP portfolio vehicle carries no statutory appraisal right unless its company or partnership agreement grants one, and the agreement may modify what it grants; a transaction in which every dissent-eligible interest is held by one owner or by owners who approved it is outside the subchapter altogether. Authority: Tex. Bus. Orgs. Code § 10.351(a)-(c). Counter: a minority LLC member points to a dissent clause imported from a corporate form into the company agreement. Defeat: read the company agreement for any dissent or appraisal grant before the vehicle is formed and price the choice of form at formation; where a corporation is required (a listing, a REIT), reserve for appraisal from day one.
21. **The 60-day petition window and the acquirer's right to file.** Mechanism: once the responsible organization rejects a demand and the § 10.358(d) period runs out, either side has 60 days to petition in the county of the principal office; the survivor may file first to consolidate every unsettled demand into one proceeding before a court that then decides which owners perfected and appoints the appraiser. Authority: § 10.361(a)-(g). Counter: the dissenter argues the § 10.358(d) period never closed because the organization's response was defective, so the 60 days never started. Defeat: the response mechanics in §§ 10.357-10.358 (Context bullets; confirm live) are the record that starts the clock; the termination consequence of a missed petition (§ 10.367(a)(3)) was read and not carried (gap entry), so confirm it live before treating a late dissenter as out; deadline-rule: pull the day count from the statute the day it matters.
22. **Fair value carries no minority or marketability haircut.** Mechanism: the court values the dissenter's interest at the going-concern value of the entity on the day before the action, excluding deal-driven appreciation or depreciation and excluding any control premium, minority ownership discount, or discount for lack of marketability, with class preferences counted and voting rights not; the number cannot be reused for any other purpose. Authority: § 10.362(a)-(c). Counter: the sponsor's expert applies a marketability discount as an input to going-concern value rather than as a separate deduction. Defeat: the quoted text bars the discount "in the computation of value", so a take-private modeled on a discounted minority price under-reserves; price the dissenting class at pro-rata going-concern value and let the (c) no-other-purpose bar keep the appraisal number out of any LP-level valuation fight.
23. **Appraisal is the minority's only remedy unless it pleads fraud.** Mechanism: absent fraud in the transaction, dissent and fair value is the exclusive remedy for the value of the interest or money damages on the action, so a dissenter cannot also sue for damages on the deal price. Authority: § 10.368; Kruse v. Henderson Texas Bancshares reads the fraud carve-out as the one question that goes to a jury. Counter: the minority pleads fraud in the transaction to escape appraisal and reach a jury. Defeat: the disclosure record built for the vote (the § 10.355 notice contents are a Context bullet; confirm live) is the fraud defense; § 10.366(b)(2), read and not carried (gap entry), preserves the fraud action, so treat every take-private disclosure as the document that decides whether the fight stays in appraisal.
24. **Appraisal is a bench proceeding.** Mechanism: in a subch. H proceeding the judge, not a jury, determines fair value: the statute speaks of a "court" and a "hearing", the 1876 jury guarantee does not reach a proceeding that did not exist then, and the proceeding is a special statutory proceeding rather than a "cause"; a court-appointed appraiser and expert evidence decide the number. Authority: Kruse v. Henderson Texas Bancshares, Inc., No. 12-18-00238-CV (Tex. App.-Tyler Aug. 30, 2019); § 10.361(e). Counter: the dissenter demands a jury in a different appellate district and argues Kruse binds only the Twelfth Court. Defeat: Kruse is a published opinion with no petition for review filed and no contrary Texas appellate decision located this pass (gap entries on Fenenbock and later citing treatment); model appraisal exposure as an expert-and-appraiser question with lower variance and cost than a jury valuation.
25. **No Delaware import into Texas director duties.** Mechanism: a sponsor-designated board of a Texas domestic entity may consider other states' law and practice and may decline to follow it, and that refusal does not constitute or imply a breach of the Code or of any Texas duty, which forecloses a fiduciary theory built on "Delaware would have required a special committee or an auction". Authority: Tex. Bus. Orgs. Code § 1.056 (S.B. 29 § 2, eff. May 14, 2025). Counter: the claimant recasts the Delaware norm as evidence of what a reasonable Texas director would do under the Texas duty of care rather than as an imported duty. Defeat: the statute reaches the failure to "conform the exercise of the managerial official's powers" to another state's law, decisions, or practices, so the practice itself cannot be the breach; pair it with the § 21.419 presumptions (angle 1) for a covered corporation; cite by caption, since a second § 1.056 (business-court references) shares the number.
26. **Fee ceilings make the filing door cheaper than the argument.** Mechanism: an original dealer or adviser application or a federal covered adviser notice filing, each renewal, and each agent or representative application or notice filing is capped at $100 by statute, with a $25 amendment or duplicate fee and one fee for a person acting concurrently as dealer and adviser or as agent and representative. Authority: Tex. Gov't Code §§ 4006.001(2)-(5), 4006.054, 4006.102(c); § 4004.052. Counter: none of substance on cost; the TSSB's counter is that a late filing is an unregistered period whatever the fee. Defeat: when the de minimis count (angle 15) nears six, notice-file or register rather than litigate the client count; cost is never the reason to skip the § 4004.052 gate; the Board-set amounts sit in the TSSB fee schedule (unfetched; indexed-figure rule) and are re-pulled at use.
27. **The management agreement creates no direct claim for individual holders.** Mechanism: an advisory or management agreement between the sponsor's management company and the entity that names "the Company and its shareholders" runs to the holders collectively, so an individual LP or minority holder cannot sue the manager directly on it; the claim belongs to the entity, runs derivatively under the BOC § 21.552 contemporaneous-ownership rule, and lands in the forum the governing documents fix; mandamus corrects a trial court that lets the individual suit proceed. Authority: In re UMTH General Services, L.P., No. 24-0024 (Tex. Nov. 14, 2025). Counter: the holder pleads an express individual undertaking in the agreement or a side letter, or argues third-party beneficiary status. Defeat: UMTH requires an express undertaking or the holder's individual assent as a party and refuses to imply third-party beneficiary status for thousands of holders; draft the management agreement with a no-third-party-beneficiary clause and a forum designation (§ 2.115, angle 3) so any derivative claim sits in the sponsor's chosen court.
