## Sponsor angles

Each angle rests only on a VERIFIED bullet above. Format: mechanism; authority; the counterparty's best counter; what defeats it.

- **Passive-entity holding vehicles** · Organize fund and holding vehicles as limited partnerships whose federal gross income is at least 90 percent dividends, interest, distributive shares, and securities gains; the vehicle is not a taxable entity and owes no margin tax. Authority: § 171.0003. Counter: the Comptroller characterizes management, monitoring, or transaction fees booked at the vehicle as active trade-or-business income above the 10 percent ceiling. Defeats it: LLC form, rental income, or fee income at the vehicle.
- **Service-receipt sourcing under Sirius XM** · Place the personnel and equipment that perform subscription, software, and media services outside Texas; receipts source to where the labor is done, not where the subscriber sits. Authority: § 171.106(a); Sirius XM. Counter: the Comptroller's post-2022 apportionment rule (34 TAC § 3.591; round 2 read only its capital-asset, intangible, and securities paragraphs on the Cornell mirror, the services-sourcing paragraphs and the official SOS record are gaps below) and the taxpayer's fair-value evidentiary burden on remand. Defeats it: Texas-based personnel or servers; internet hosting receipts, which § 171.106(g) sources to the customer's location regardless.
- **Economic nexus at $500,000** · An out-of-state portfolio company with $500,000 of Texas-sourced receipts owes a franchise report with no Texas office or employees. Authority: 34 TAC § 3.586(f). Counter: none; this is exposure. Manage it by tracking Texas-sourced receipts and filing; § 171.1014(i) joint and several liability spreads a missed filing across the combined group.
- **Combined-group diligence and the single election** · A target that joins the sponsor's unitary Texas group shares one COGS-or-compensation election and joint and several liability for the group's tax. Authority: § 171.1014(b), (d), (i); § 171.101(d). Counter: a seller argues no unitary business exists, keeping histories separate. Defeats it: an integrated operating platform after close. Underwrite the target's prior-period franchise exposure and take a specific indemnity.
- **Rate and base arbitrage** · Retail or wholesale platforms meeting the § 171.002(c) test pay 0.375 percent; information and data processing services charge sales tax on 80 percent of the price under § 151.351; the $480,000 per-person compensation cap for 2026-2027 reports limits the compensation deduction on executive-heavy payrolls. Authority: § 171.002(a)-(c); § 151.351; Comptroller 2026-2027 table. Counter: audit of the retail/wholesale classification and of the data-processing characterization. Defeats it: mixed-activity revenue that fails the three-part test; a payroll so top-heavy that COGS becomes the better election.
- **Subchapter T research credit** · 8.722 percent of qualified research expenses above half the three-period average, 10.903 percent with a Texas higher-education contract, from 2026 reports. Authority: § 171.9204; S.B. 2206. Counter: audit of what counts as qualified research. Defeats it: no prior-period baseline, which drops the rate to 4.361 or 5.451 percent of current expenses.
- **Business personal property and the expiring circuit breaker** · $125,000 of income-producing tangible personal property per location is exempt from 2026; related entities in a unified enterprise at one location are aggregated, so splitting assets among affiliates does not multiply the exemption. The 20 percent appraisal cap on non-homestead real property at or below the indexed ceiling expires December 31, 2026 with no extension enacted. Authority: § 11.145(b), (c), (f); § 23.231(b), (d), (k). Counter: the appraisal district treats affiliates as one enterprise. Defeats it: 2027 real-estate underwriting that assumes the cap survives; it is gone unless the 2027 Legislature acts.
- **Individual-level shield for Texas-resident principals** · No state tax on distributive shares, carried interest allocations, or realized or unrealized capital gains at the individual, family, estate, or trust level. Authority: art. VIII, §§ 24-a, 24-b. Counter: another state's residency or source claim on the same gain; federal treatment is untouched. Defeats it: domicile outside Texas.
- **JETI school M&O limitation** · Qualifying projects pay school maintenance and operations tax on 50 percent of market value (25 percent in a qualified opportunity zone) for each year of the incentive period. Authority: Gov't Code § 403.605. Counter: the jobs, investment, and project-type gates in §§ 403.602 and 403.604 (CONTEXT bullets, unverified). Defeats it: a project outside the eligible categories.
- **Asset-deal tax clearance and holdback** · File the § 111.020(c) joint request for a certificate of no tax due, or a statement of the amount owed, at signing so the 60-day and 90-day clocks run out before closing, and hold back purchase price until the certificate or the (d) release arrives; withholding is mandatory on any sale of the business or its stock of goods, and a buyer that closes without it is liable up to the purchase price. An equity purchase leaves the taxpayer entity in place, so (a) is not triggered and the historical exposure stays inside the target for indemnity pricing. Authority: § 111.020(a)-(d). Counter: the seller resists the joint request because the Comptroller may audit before issuing, stretching to the 90-day outer limit. Defeats it: closing before the certificate or the (d) release; the Comptroller's reading that assumed indebtedness counts toward the cap (publication 98-117, agency gloss).
- **Fraudulent-transfer exposure on distressed carve-outs** · A purchase from a delinquent seller at a thin price, by an insider or by a buyer that keeps the seller's management, location, and operations running as one business, fits several § 111.024(c) intent factors and exposes the buyer to the seller's tax, penalty, and interest with no purchase-price cap of the § 111.020(b) kind; paper the reasonably equivalent value and arm's-length terms before signing. Authority: § 111.024(a)-(c); § 111.020(b). Counter: the Comptroller assesses under § 111.024 despite withholding when the price is not reasonably equivalent (§ 111.020(f), CONTEXT bullet, unverified). Defeats it: a valuation file supporting the price and a transfer to an unrelated buyer that does not continue the seller's operation as one enterprise.
- **Occasional-sale exemption on the asset purchase** · Structure the transfer as the sale of the entire operating assets of the business, or of a division, branch, or segment whose income and expenses were separately ascertainable on the seller's books before the sale, so no sales tax falls on the tangible assets conveyed. Authority: § 151.304(a), (b)(2), (c). Counter: the Comptroller applies its rule reading operating assets as tangible personal property used exclusively by the enterprise, excluding inventory and intangibles, and requiring a single transaction to a single purchaser (34 TAC § 3.316, CONTEXT bullets, unverified). Defeats it: a carve-out with no standalone books; staggered closings or multiple buyer entities if the rule-level single-transaction reading holds.
- **Exit gains and payor domicile** · An exit inside a Texas-filing taxable entity adds only the net gain, not gross proceeds, to both sides of the apportionment fraction, and under the Comptroller's rule the gain on stock or an LLC interest sources to the buyer's legal domicile: a buyer domiciled outside Texas keeps the gain out of the Texas numerator, a Texas-domiciled buyer pulls it in. Authority: § 171.105(b); 34 TAC § 3.591(b)(8), (e)(2)(A), (D), (e)(21)(B), (e)(27). Counter: the Comptroller's announced but unexecuted § 3.591 amendment (Tax Policy News, April 2026) and the 8.7 percent exchange fallback where the payor cannot be identified (§ 3.591(e)(25), CONTEXT bullet, unverified). Defeats it: a Texas-domiciled acquirer; a security treated as inventory or as available-for-sale or trading under FAS 115, which § 171.106(f), (f-1) count at gross proceeds.
- **No deference in Comptroller disputes** · A challenge to a Comptroller rule or rule-based assessment in a proceeding initiated on or after September 1, 2025, including a § 2001.038 rule-validity action against § 3.591 sourcing or § 3.316 occasional-sale readings, starts without deference to the agency's legal determination. Authority: Gov't Code § 2001.042. Counter: the court may still consider a reasonable agency determination that does not conflict with plain statutory language. Defeats it: a dispute that turns on facts rather than on the construction, validity, or applicability of the law or rule; a proceeding initiated before September 1, 2025, which follows prior law under enrolled SECTION 11.
- **Forfeiture status diligence and the 45-day and 120-day clocks** · Pull the Comptroller account status and the Secretary of State record for every Texas-registered target and sponsor vehicle; a missed May 15 report or payment forfeits privileges 45 days after the Comptroller's notice, and an entity still unrevived 120 days after that forfeiture loses its charter or registration administratively, with no court involved, so a target whose SOS record shows a forfeiture needs reinstatement before closing and a representation that no debts were incurred after the missed due date. Authority: § 171.251(1)-(3); § 171.2515(a)-(b); § 171.309(1)-(2); § 171.310; Comptroller 2026-2027 table (May 15 due date). Counter: the seller treats the lapse as clerical and offers to cure after closing. Defeats it: closing before revival, which leaves the officer and director exposure of the next angle running through the gap; the cure cost (all delinquent tax, penalty, and interest) left out of the purchase price adjustment.
- **Officer and director exposure during a Texas delinquency** · Each director or officer of a delinquent Texas taxable entity, corporate or not, is personally liable as a partner would be for each debt created or incurred in Texas from the missed due date until revival, including franchise tax and penalties that come due after forfeiture, so sponsor appointees on a portfolio company board carry vendor, lender, and tax debt incurred during the gap. Authority: § 171.255(a)-(b); § 171.2515(b). Counter: a creditor invokes the liability after the entity is revived, since revival leaves incurred liability in place (§ 171.255(d), CONTEXT bullet, unverified). Defeats it: written board objections to new debt while any Texas entity is delinquent and the no-knowledge showing (§ 171.255(c), CONTEXT bullet, unverified); a D&O and indemnity review for tax-forfeiture carve-outs; cure before the entity incurs debt. How "director or officer" maps onto LLC managers and LP general partners under § 171.2515 was not tested against any opinion (gap below).
- **Management-fee sourcing by location of performance** · Under the Comptroller's post-Sirius XM rule a management company's or GP's fee is a service receipt sourced to where its personnel or property do the hired work, not to the fund's or the limited partners' domicile; an out-of-state deal team keeps the fee out of the Texas numerator, a Texas-based team pulls it in. Authority: 34 TAC § 3.591(e)(26)(A); § 171.106(a); Sirius XM. Counter: the Comptroller applies the fair-value split for a single charge covering work inside and outside Texas and its fallback measures where no location records exist (§ 3.591(e)(26)(B), CONTEXT bullet, unverified). Defeats it: Texas-based investment professionals; no time or value records by location; a regulated investment company client, whose fees run through the separate § 171.106(b) fraction rather than the general services rule.
- **E-Z election for sub-$20 million vehicles** · A management company or holding vehicle with total revenue of not more than $20 million may elect 0.331 percent of apportioned total revenue with no COGS, compensation, credit, or other adjustment; run it against 0.75 percent of margin each report year, since the election bars the Subchapter T research credit and every other adjustment for that report. Authority: § 171.1016(a)-(c); § 171.002(a); § 171.9204. Counter: none; the election is made report by report and the entity's own arithmetic decides it. Defeats it: total revenue above $20 million; a margin thin enough after the § 171.101 deductions that 0.75 percent of margin beats 0.331 percent of revenue.
- **Excluded receipts drop out of both apportionment factors** · Receipts excluded from total revenue under § 171.1011 and intercompany receipts eliminated under § 171.1014(c)(3) are counted in neither Texas receipts nor everywhere receipts, so distributive-share and passive-entity income that leaves total revenue neither dilutes nor inflates the Texas percentage applied to a GP's remaining fee receipts. Authority: § 171.1055(a)-(c); § 171.1014(c)(3). Counter: the Comptroller characterizes the receipt as fee or active income that stays in total revenue and in the fraction (the § 171.1011 net distributive income and passive-entity exclusions are CONTEXT bullets, unverified). Defeats it: the (b) drop-shipment exception for tangible personal property resold into Texas by a member without nexus; income that never qualified for a § 171.1011 exclusion.
- **Local sales tax by place of business** · A portfolio company selling from a single Texas place of business collects that location's local rate on every Texas sale regardless of where title or possession passes; adding a second Texas order desk or warehouse re-sources sales to where the order is first received in person, then to the ship-from or possession location, and changes both the local rate and the receiving jurisdiction. Authority: § 321.203(a)-(d); § 323.203(a)-(m). Counter: the destination rules for itinerant vendors, out-of-state order-takers, drop shipments, marketplace sales, and nonresidential remodeling at the job site (§ 321.203(e), (e-1), (n), CONTEXT bullet, unverified); 89R HB 134 would have rewritten sourcing to destination and stopped in Calendars on 04/09/2025. Defeats it: an order-taking or fulfillment footprint spread across Texas jurisdictions; Comptroller rule 34 TAC § 3.334, not fetched.
