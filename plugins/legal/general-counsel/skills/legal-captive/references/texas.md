## Chapter 964 scope: one captive type

- **Tex. Ins. Code § 964.001(a)(2)** · Texas recognizes one captive type: a company holding a TDI certificate of authority to insure the operational risks of its affiliates or the risks of a controlled unaffiliated business; the term includes a captive exchange (reciprocal). Chapter 964 defines no association, agency, industrial-insured, sponsored, or cell captive. ""Captive insurance company" means a company that holds a certificate of authority under this chapter to insure the operational risks of the company's affiliates or risks of a controlled unaffiliated business. The term includes a captive exchange." https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/IN/htm/IN.964.htm (verified 2026-09-16; official text current through 89th 2nd C.S. 2025; added S.B. 734 (2013), operative text H.B. 1944 § 1.01 eff. June 15, 2017, unamended since; Justia 2025 mirror identical) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. Ins. Code § 964.001(a)(5); § 964.066** · A controlled unaffiliated business is a non-affiliate with an existing contractual relationship with an affiliate under which the affiliate bears potential financial loss, whose risks the captive manages; § 964.066 lets the commissioner adopt control standards and states "Until rules under this section are adopted, the commissioner may approve the coverage of these risks." "a person: (A) that is not an affiliate; (B) that has an existing contractual relationship with an affiliate under which the affiliate bears a potential financial loss; and (C) the risks of which are managed by a captive insurance company" https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/IN/htm/IN.964.htm (verified 2026-09-16; § 964.001(a)(5) last amended H.B. 1944 eff. June 15, 2017; § 964.066 shows only its 2013 enactment; official text current through 89th 2nd C.S. 2025) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. Ins. Code § 964.051(a)** · A Texas captive may write any type of insurance but may insure only the operational risks of its affiliates and the risks of a controlled unaffiliated business; the sentence opens "Except as provided by this section," pointing to the (b)-(c) prohibitions. "a captive insurance company may write any type of insurance, but may only insure the operational risks of the company's affiliates and risks of a controlled unaffiliated business." https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/IN/htm/IN.964.htm (verified 2026-09-16; added 2013, amended H.B. 1944 eff. June 15, 2017, nothing later; official text current through 89th 2nd C.S. 2025) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. Ins. Code § 964.051(b)-(d)** · Prohibited under (b): life insurance, and accident and health insurance for the parent and affiliates (each except ERISA-subject employee benefits), annuities, title, mortgage guaranty, financial guaranty, residential property, personal automobile, and workers' compensation. Prohibited under (c): any insurance, including automobile liability, that state or political-subdivision law requires as a license or permit prerequisite where the law requires an authorized insurer. Permitted under (d): a contractual reimbursement policy to an affiliated certified self-insurer under Labor Code ch. 407 (or an analogous other-state entity) or to an affiliate insured under a workers' compensation policy with a negotiated deductible endorsement; the statute says "negotiated deductible," not "large deductible." "(4) title insurance; (5) mortgage guaranty insurance; (6) financial guaranty insurance; (7) residential property insurance; (8) personal automobile insurance; or (9) workers' compensation insurance." https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/IN/htm/IN.964.htm (verified 2026-09-16; (b) text from H.B. 1944 § 1.02 eff. June 15, 2017, enrolled bill fetched; official text current through 89th 2nd C.S. 2025) [VERIFIED]

## Formation and certificate of authority

- **Tex. Ins. Code § 964.053(a)-(b)** · Formation in any Business Organizations Code form except a risk retention group or general partnership; nonprofit form only for a captive controlled by a nonprofit corporation; a captive other than a captive exchange or an attorney in fact forms by filing with the secretary of state. "A captive insurance company may be formed and operated in any form of business organization authorized under the Business Organizations Code except a risk retention group or general partnership." https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/IN/htm/IN.964.htm (verified 2026-09-16; added 2013, (a) amended H.B. 1944 § 1.04 eff. June 15, 2017, nothing later; Justia 2025 mirror identical) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. Ins. Code § 964.055(a)-(b)** · No entity may operate as a Texas-domiciled captive without a TDI certificate of authority (a). To qualify: (b)(1) its affiliates have significant operations in Texas as the commissioner determines; (b)(2) its board or governing body meets in Texas at least once a year; (b)(3) it keeps its principal office and books and records in Texas unless TDI approves relocation under ch. 803; and (b)(4) it complies with § 804.101 or § 804.102, a fourth condition both verifiers saw in the text (§§ 804.101-.102 themselves not fetched). "its affiliates have significant operations in this state, as determined by the commissioner; (2) its board of directors or governing body holds at least one meeting each year in this state" https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/IN/htm/IN.964.htm (verified 2026-09-16; (a) amended H.B. 1944 § 1.05 eff. ## Capital, distributions, and TDI's dividend rule

- **Tex. Ins. Code § 964.056(a)-(b)** · TDI may not issue a certificate unless the captive holds unencumbered capital and surplus in an amount the commissioner sets after weighing premium written, asset characteristics, reinsurance terms, business type, underwriting practices, and other criteria (a); the amount may not be less than $250,000 (b). "The amount of capital and surplus, or the equivalent, determined by the commissioner under Subsection (a) may not be less than $250,000." https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/IN/htm/IN.964.htm (verified 2026-09-16; section rewritten by H.B. 1944 § 1.06 eff. - **Tex. Ins. Code § 964.063(a)-(b)** · Written notice to the commissioner of policyholder dividends or distributions (a); dividends or distributions to equity holders only with the commissioner's approval, with a rulemaking directive (b). "A captive insurance company, with the commissioner's approval, may issue dividends or distributions to the holders of an equity interest in the captive insurance company. The commissioner shall adopt rules to implement this subsection." https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/IN/htm/IN.964.htm (verified 2026-09-16; (b) enacted S.B. 667 § 3 eff. ## Premium tax on licensed captives (ch. 223A)

- **Tex. Ins. Code § 223A.003(a)-(d)** · Annual premium tax on each licensed captive at one-half percent of taxable premium receipts (a); receipts include all premiums, fees, dues, and other consideration for insurance written without regard to risk location (b); excluded are reinsurance premiums from authorized insurers, returned premiums, policyholder dividends, and premiums other law excludes (c); no deduction for reinsurance ceded (d). "The rate of the tax is one-half percent of the company's taxable premium receipts for a calendar year." https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/insurance-code/title-3/subtitle-b/chapter-223a/section-223a-003/ (verified 2026-09-16; Justia page labeled 2025 Texas Statutes, matched verbatim against official IN.223A current through 89th 2nd C.S. 2025; source note shows only S.B. 734 eff. June 14, 2013) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. Ins. Code § 223A.003(e)** · Annual minimum $7,500 and annual maximum $200,000; premiums taxed under ch. 223A are exempt from other Insurance Code taxes, surcharges, assessments, and fees except the maintenance tax under § 964.068. "The annual minimum aggregate tax to be paid by a captive insurance company under this chapter is $7,500 and the annual maximum aggregate tax to be paid by a company under this chapter is $200,000." https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/insurance-code/title-3/subtitle-b/chapter-223a/section-223a-003/ (verified 2026-09-16; verbatim on the official site and the Justia 2025 mirror, unamended since 2013; § 964.068 unchanged since 2013; Comptroller captive.php states the same figures) [VERIFIED]
- **Tex. Ins. Code § 223A.004** · Tax due March 1 after the calendar year (a); a captive whose prior-year net liability exceeded $1,000 prepays 50% of the prior year's tax on March 1 and August 1 (b); the comptroller refunds overpayments (c). "The total tax imposed by this chapter is due and payable not later than March 1 after the end of the calendar year for which the tax is due." https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/insurance-code/title-3/subtitle-b/chapter-223a/section-223a-004/ (verified 2026-09-16; verbatim on the official site current through 89th 2nd C.S. 2025 and the Justia 2025 mirror; only the 2013 enactment in the source note; Comptroller captive.php lists the same dates) [VERIFIED]

## Texas case law

- **SideCars, Inc. v. Tex. Dep't of Ins., No. 03-10-00720-CV (Tex. ## Sponsor angles

Each angle rests only on VERIFIED bullets above. Comparators, confidentiality, and intercompany loans are CONTEXT and carry no angle until verified; the redomestication waiver angle below rests on TDI's VERIFIED July 2026 proposal and 2026 rule index, not on the CONTEXT § 964.071 or § 6.601 text.

**The pure-captive perimeter and the controlled-unaffiliated-business door.** Mechanism: portfolio companies enter as affiliates; a subcontractor, vendor, or customer enters only as a controlled unaffiliated business, which requires an existing contract under which an affiliate bears potential financial loss and captive management of that party's risk, and until TDI adopts control standards the commissioner approves such coverage case by case. Authority: § 964.001(a)(2), (a)(5); § 964.051(a); § 964.066. Counter: TDI reads "managed by a captive insurance company" narrowly or finds the affiliate's contractual loss exposure thin and withholds § 964.066 approval. What defeats it: write the loss-bearing contract term and the captive's risk-management role into the record before the application is filed and request approval on that record; the definition is the test and approval is discretionary, not foreclosed. The statutory definition of "affiliate" (§ 964.001(a)(1), incorporating § 823.003) is VERIFIED above under "Definitions and Code applicability"; the control-chain angle below applies it to a co-investor's company.

**Workers' compensation through the deductible door.** Mechanism: the captive cannot write workers' compensation, but § 964.051(d) authorizes a contractual reimbursement policy issued to an affiliated certified self-insurer under Labor Code ch. 407 or to an affiliate insured under a workers' compensation policy carrying a negotiated deductible endorsement; the authorized carrier stays on the risk and the deductible layer sits in the captive. Authority: § 964.051(b)(9), (c), (d). Counter: TDI or the fronting carrier characterizes the reimbursement policy as workers' compensation barred by (b)(9) or as a license-prerequisite coverage caught by (c). What defeats it: (d) is the express carve-out from (b) and (c), the policy is issued to the affiliate as (d) prescribes, and the statute names "negotiated deductible," so the endorsement language in the carrier policy is the document to get right. Labor Code ch. 407 was not fetched.

**Capital: a statutory floor and a negotiable figure above it.** Mechanism: TDI's number above $250,000 turns on the § 964.056(a) factors (premium written, asset characteristics, reinsurance terms, business type, underwriting practices, other criteria); the sponsor who arrives with the actuarial case on the five named factors frames the sixth. Ongoing, 28 TAC § 6.301 requires at least $100,000 in annual premium and an affiliate or controlling person with the wherewithal to keep placing risk. Authority: § 964.056(a)-(b); 28 TAC § 6.301(2)-(3), (7). Counter: TDI invokes "other criteria" and premium volume to set capital far above the floor, or later revokes under § 964.065(9) when writings slip under $100,000. What defeats it: the open "other criteria" clause is TDI's room and the five named factors are the sponsor's; size year-one premium above $100,000 and document the controlling person's funding capacity in the application projections.

**Distributions run on a 30-day regulatory clock.** Mechanism: every dividend or distribution to equity holders needs the commissioner's approval before payment plus at least 30 days' prior notice stating amount, declaration and payment dates, and writings-to-surplus and reserves-to-surplus ratios before and after; policyholder dividends are reported within 30 days after issuance. Authority: § 964.063(a)-(b); 28 TAC § 6.409(a)-(b). Counter: TDI withholds or delays approval of a distribution the fund has already scheduled. What defeats it: the rule enumerates the notice content, so a notice carrying every item with ratios that hold post-payment starts the clock complete on day one; calendar fund distributions at least 30 days behind the notice and sequence a large return of surplus across more than one approved notice.

**Reinsurance credit and the $250 million / B+ bar.** Mechanism: reserve credit on a cession to a nonaffiliated reinsurer requires an NAIC-qualified-jurisdiction certificate acceptable to the commissioner, $250 million in capital and surplus at the prior year-end, and a B+ (or equivalent) rating from an NRSRO acceptable to the commissioner, or the cession qualifies under Subchapter C, Chapter 493; cessions to or from affiliated captives or pools require the commissioner's approval; each agreement and each termination triggers a 30-day notice. Authority: § 964.052(b)-(f); § 964.072. Counter: TDI denies reserve credit for a fronting or quota-share partner below the bar, or declines to approve a cross-captive pool. What defeats it: clear (f) on the reinsurer's prior year-end figures before signing; for an affiliated pool, file the § 964.072 approval request ahead of execution so the 30-day post-execution notice follows an approval. Subchapter C, Chapter 493 was not fetched; ground it before relying on the alternate path.

**Licensed at 0.5% or nonadmitted at 4.85%, and who is liable.** Mechanism: a Texas-licensed captive pays one-half percent of worldwide gross premium with no reinsurance deduction, $7,500 minimum, $200,000 maximum (reached at $40 million of taxable premium, arithmetic on the verified rate); a nonadmitted sponsor captive covering Texas-situated risk pays 4.85% on the Texas-allocated gross premium, in lieu of other insurance taxes, and if unpaid the insured portfolio company is liable alongside the insurer and agent. § 101.053(b)(6) spares the affiliate-only nonadmitted captive from TDI licensure and spares it nothing on tax. Authority: § 223A.003(a)-(e); § 223A.004; §§ 226.003(a), (c), (g)(3), 226.004, 226.005; § 101.053(b)(6), (c). Counter: the Comptroller assesses the 4.85% against the Texas portfolio company under § 226.005 when the out-of-state captive never filed; TDI denies Texas licensure because the insured affiliates lack significant Texas operations under § 964.055(b)(1). What defeats it: with real Texas operations, license in Texas and pay ch. 223A, which exempts the premium from every other Insurance Code tax except the § 964.068 maintenance tax; without a Texas footprint, price the 4.85% into the Texas allocation and file it, because the liability lands on the insured either way.

**The 2026 amnesty window.** Mechanism: the Comptroller's July 2026 announcement lets a nonadmitted captive and its insureds file Forms 25-108 and 25-123 for tax years 2022-2025 by December 31, 2026, with a four-year lookback and penalties and interest waived; the Comptroller is contacting franchise taxpayers it believes are affiliated with nonadmitted captives. Authority: Comptroller Tax Policy News (July 2026). Counter: the Comptroller's notice reaches the sponsor's portfolio company first, or the announcement's unstated statutory basis lets a later position narrow it. What defeats it: file before the notice arrives and before December 31, 2026; the announcement is the Comptroller's own published position and names the forms, so the compliance record exists on the sponsor's timetable. The statutory or rule authority behind the waiver was not located.

**A static chapter.** Mechanism: ch. 964's operative text dates to 2013-2017, the 89th Legislature enacted nothing touching it, no 2026 session has sat, and the next regular session opens January 12, 2027. Authority: ch. 964 source notes and the statutes.capitol.texas.gov currency statement; LRL Legislative Sessions and Years table. Counter: TDI rulemaking under 28 TAC ch. 6 moves without a statute (a § 6.601 item is pending per a verifier note; see gaps). What defeats it: the statutory frame a memo rests on is fixed through at least January 2027; read TDI's ch. 6 rule index before each filing.

**Affiliate status runs on the § 823.003 control chain, and control can come by contract.** Mechanism: a portfolio company is an affiliate of the captive only if it controls, is controlled by, or is under common control with the captive through one or more intermediaries (§ 823.003(a), incorporated by § 964.001(a)(1)), or is the controlling parent that (a)(1) adds; control is the power to direct management and policies by any means including voting securities or contract, excluding power that comes from an office held and excluding a commercial contract for goods or nonmanagement services (§ 964.001(a)(4)). Common control through the fund or its general partner is what makes every controlled portfolio company an affiliate of a single Texas captive; a minority position without control breaks the chain, and a co-investor's separate company with no control link enters, if at all, only as a controlled unaffiliated business under § 964.001(a)(5). Authority: § 964.001(a)(1), (a)(4), (a)(5); § 823.003(a)-(c). Counter: TDI treats a management or services agreement as a commercial contract for nonmanagement services and denies contract-based control, or locates the sponsor's control in officers' positions rather than in an entity. What defeats it: the (a)(4) exclusion reaches only nonmanagement services, so a services agreement that directs the portfolio company's policies is the document to draft with the control language on its face; ch. 964 states no percentage presumption, so the record, not a threshold, carries the point. The 10 percent presumption in § 823.151 sits in ch. 823 and is not incorporated by (a)(4).

**Operational risk: any loss of an affiliate, but not an affiliated insurer's policies.** Mechanism: § 964.051(a) lets the captive insure operational risks, defined as any potential financial loss of an affiliate except a loss arising from an insurance policy issued by a captive or insurance affiliate (§ 964.001(a)(6)); the risk side is open and the insured side is closed to affiliates. Authority: § 964.001(a)(6); § 964.051(a). Counter: a sponsor group holding a licensed insurer tries to place that insurer's policy obligations in the captive as direct coverage. What defeats it: the carve-out bars the direct route; the reinsurance route under § 964.052 (30-day notice of each agreement and termination, commissioner approval for affiliated cessions under (e) and § 964.072) is the path that stays inside the chapter.

**What the Code leaves behind, and when ch. 823 comes back.** Mechanism: § 964.002(a) switches the Insurance Code off for a captive except Title 2, ch. 223A with Title 3 Subtitles A and C, and chs. 401, 441, 443, and 803; § 964.002(c) applies ch. 823 only if the captive is affiliated with another insurer that is itself subject to ch. 823; § 964.002(b) keeps the Business Organizations Code, including secretary of state authorization. Authority: § 964.002(a)-(c). Counter: a sponsor group that acquires or already holds a licensed insurer pulls the captive into ch. 823 through (c), with the holding-company filings that follow. What defeats it: map the control chain before formation; a structure with no licensed insurer in it stays outside ch. 823 entirely, and one with an insurer prices the ch. 823 compliance line from day one. The carve-in list is the statute's; the chapter labels for chs. 441 and 443 are the index's and were not verified.

**"Solely" means solely: the nonadmitted exemption and third-party benefit.** Mechanism: § 101.053(b)(6) exempts a nonadmitted captive that insures solely its parent's and affiliates' risks; in SideCars the Third Court held the exemption inapplicable as a matter of law where the program's written notice promised payment measured by the customer's vehicle and loan, because that coverage did not insure solely the dealer's risk. Authority: § 101.053(b)(6), (c); SideCars, Inc. v. Tex. Dep't of Ins., No. 03-10-00720-CV (Tex. App.-Austin May 30, 2013, pet. denied) (mem. op.). Counter: a sponsor's out-of-state captive covering Texas risk writes a coverage that a customer, buyer, or other third party can claim under, and TDI treats the arrangement as unauthorized insurance outside the §§ 101.051-.052 exemption. What defeats it: draft the policy so the only insured and the only payee is the affiliate, with no promise running to a third party. SideCars is a memorandum opinion decided before ch. 964 took effect, with no citing opinions on CourtListener; it is the only appellate reading of (b)(6) found and is not a ch. 964 holding.

**The ch. 6 rulebook is 2018 vintage.** Mechanism: TDI's last adopted ch. 6 rulemaking took effect December 30, 2018 (Order 2018-5738, amending §§ 6.1, 6.2, 6.202, 6.203, 6.302, 6.303, 6.401, 6.409 and adding § 6.411 with the revised Texas Captive Annual Report); the 2019-2025 rule indexes list no ch. 6 item and the only 2026 item is the unadopted § 6.601 proposal. Authority: Commissioner's Order No. 2018-5738; TDI rule indexes 2018-2026. Counter: TDI revises the annual report or an application form outside rulemaking, or adopts the § 6.601 amendment after the September 14, 2026 index update. What defeats it: the rule text a filing rests on is fixed as of December 2018 and the forms are the variable; pull the current form from TDI's captive page at filing time and read the 2026 rule index for a § 6.601 adoption before any redomestication request.

**S.B. 1455 reaches the captive by cross-reference only.** Mechanism: the 2025 act, effective January 1, 2026, converts the workers' compensation maintenance tax into surcharges, renames Title 3 and Subtitle C of the Insurance Code to add "surcharges," and amends thirty Insurance Code and Labor Code provisions, none in ch. 964 or ch. 223A; § 964.068 still imposes the captive's maintenance tax "under Subtitle C, Title 3" on the lines the captive writes, with no 2025 source note, and § 964.051(b)(9) bars the captive from writing workers' compensation, so no captive premium sits in a surcharged line. Authority: S.B. 1455 enrolled §§ 1-2, 1-30, 32; § 964.068; § 964.051(b)-(d); TLO History for S.B. 1455. Counter: a reviewer reads the renamed Subtitle C heading, or TDI's addition of "surcharges" to 28 TAC § 6.601, as a new levy on the captive, or the fronting carrier behind a § 964.051(d) reimbursement policy prices its surcharge into the deductible program. What defeats it: the captive's tax line is the § 964.068 text, unchanged since 2013, applied line by line to what the captive writes; the surcharge falls on the affiliate's authorized WC carrier, whose policy the captive reimburses but does not write, so the memo footnote cites the enrolled § 32 date and the codified Acts 2025, Ch. 358 source note, and any surcharge pass-through is negotiated in the fronting agreement rather than conceded as a captive tax.

**The redomestication waiver menu is about to grow one item.** Mechanism: a foreign or alien captive redomesticating to Texas may ask the commissioner to postpone or waive taxes and fees for up to two tax reporting years at the commissioner's sole discretion; TDI's July 16, 2026 proposal adds "(4) workers' compensation surcharges" to that menu, retitles § 6.601, and changes nothing else, and as of TDI's September 14, 2026 index and the September 11, 2026 Texas Register the amendment is proposed, not adopted. Authority: TDI § 6.601 proposal (July 16, 2026); TDI Proposed and Adopted Rules for 2026; 51 Tex. Reg. No. 37 table of contents. Counter: TDI adopts between the request and the decision and reads a request that named only the items in the current rule as waiving nothing else, or treats the surcharge item as irrelevant to a captive that writes no workers' compensation. What defeats it: file the waiver request with the application naming every item the current rule lists, cite TDI's own statement that S.B. 1455 changed only terminology, and reserve the surcharge item pending adoption; read the 2026 rule index and the Texas Register adopted-rules pages for Title 28 on the filing date, since the earliest issue that could carry the adoption is September 18, 2026.

**First impression means the approval letter is the precedent.** Mechanism: no Texas appellate court has construed any provision of ch. 964; the only appellate opinion citing the chapter (Poolre, 2025) borrows § 964.001's definition for Bahamas captives redomiciled to Delaware, and CourtListener searches across the Texas Supreme Court, Court of Criminal Appeals, courts of appeals, the four Texas federal districts, and the Fifth Circuit return no decision on a Texas-licensed captive. Every discretionary call the chapter hands the commissioner (capital above the floor under § 964.056(a), equity distributions under § 964.063(b), controlled-unaffiliated-business coverage under § 964.066, affiliated cessions under § 964.052(e) and § 964.072) therefore has no judicial gloss; TDI's written approvals are the only reading of the statute that exists. Authority: CourtListener v4 search bullet under "Texas case law: ch. 964 search, round 4"; § 964.056(a)-(b); § 964.063(a)-(b); § 964.001(a)(5) and § 964.066; § 964.052(d)-(f) and § 964.072. Counter: TDI's position shifts between filings, and with no case to cite the sponsor has no external check on a narrower reading. What defeats it: request every approval in writing on a record that states the statutory test the sponsor relies on, keep each approval letter as the file precedent for the next filing, and treat the absence bound (CourtListener's index; no full-text search at search.txcourts.gov; Westlaw and Lexis unqueried) as a reason to re-run the search before any memo asserts first impression.
